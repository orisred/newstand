### Offline tokens moved from KV to D1 (2026-11-03)

Background jobs depend on Shopify's expiring offline tokens, so Step 3 tested them on `newstand-dev`.

- **Safe:** two refreshes at once, or a refresh from a stale read. The old refresh token keeps working until its successor is used, and it returns the same successor.
- **Safe, as far as tested:** two token exchanges at once. In 2 of 2 rounds on a cold token they returned the same token, and in 3 more on a fresh one both results still refreshed. Both used one ID token, because App Bridge returned the same one. Shopify doesn't document this, so it could change. It's why nothing stops the admin's parallel loaders from each running an exchange on first load.
- **Not safe:** a refresh racing a token exchange. In 2 of 3 rounds both returned 200 but the refresh's new refresh token was already dead, and in the third the refresh itself got a 401. If the dead token is stored last, jobs fail once the access token expires, until the merchant next opens the app. An exchange on a token that has gone cold also retires the previous refresh token straight away.

The library ran an exchange whenever an admin request found a token within 5 minutes of expiry, and a job refreshing at that moment could hit this race. So `app/session-storage.server.ts` now refreshes on load, for admin requests and jobs alike, and an exchange only happens when there's no session or Shopify rejects the refresh token. This departs from Shopify's advice to use a token exchange during an active merchant session, because that advice is what makes the race possible.

Sessions moved to D1 because the KV adapter doesn't fit refreshing: its per-shop list grows on every store and is read key by key on uninstall, and KV limits writes to one per second per key, which two parallel loaders can exceed. D1 has neither problem and no stale reads. The cost is one small D1 read per `authenticate.admin` call.

### Plans and selling plans (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 5 that Steps 6, 7 and 9 live with.

- **One selling plan group per plan, holding one selling plan**, attached to one product. The group's `merchantCode` is the plan's ID, which is how a sync finds a group whose ID never reached D1. Shopify's admin shows that code as the group's title on the product (cosmetic).
- **Customers see `displayName`** as both the group and the selling plan name; the group's option is named "Subscription".
- **Price:** a fixed `PRICE` pricing policy, so the plan's price replaces the product's at checkout. The selling plan's currency is the store's, read at sync time and stored on the plan.
- **Schedule:** Shopify never bills a contract by itself, only a `subscriptionBillingAttemptCreate` from the app does, and the delivery policy only labels the contract. So the intervals are chosen to be harmless: a bundle uses its term (`YEAR` x years if the term is whole years, else `MONTH` x months), so the contract's next billing date is the term date; a per-release plan uses `YEAR` x 1. No anchors, cutoff, minimum or maximum cycles. The delivery policy is `FULFILLMENT_BEGIN` with `ASAP`. **Verified live in Step 6 (two test checkouts):** both contracts read back `billingPolicy` and `deliveryPolicy` as `YEAR` x 1 with no anchors or cycle limits, and `nextBillingDate` is one year after checkout, at 16:00 UTC (11:00 in the store's New York time) rather than the checkout time. Step 9 confirmed a billing attempt outside the cycle date is accepted (see "Renewals and charges").
- **Group `appId`:** every group carries `appId: "release-subscriptions"` (`SELLING_PLAN_APP_ID`). Liquid exposes it as `selling_plan_group.app_id`, which is how the theme block shows only this app's plans; it was `null` before, so there was no other way to tell the app's groups apart. A sync puts it on groups made earlier.
- **One-time purchases:** while a product has a plan attached, the sync sets `requiresSellingPlan` on it, so Shopify refuses a cart without a plan (checked: add to cart and a `/cart/<variant>:1` link both fail). When a plan leaves a product that now has no selling plan group at all, the sync sets it back to false, because a product that requires a plan and has none can't be bought. A product that still has any group (this app's or another's) keeps it.
- **Shippable check:** the sync reports MISMATCH when a variant of the product isn't a physical product (`requiresShipping` false), because checkout then collects no shipping address (checked live: only a billing address was asked). The app doesn't change the variant.
- **Contract facts (Step 6, test gateway, guest checkout):** the contract is `ACTIVE`, its line's `sellingPlanId` is the plan's selling plan, and its `originOrder` is the checkout order: `PAID`, `UNFULFILLED`, with a `SHIPPING` fulfillment order `OPEN` at the shop location (the app must mark it fulfilled in Step 7). The order's line item has the plan too. The store's customer accounts setting is optional, and checkout worked as a guest, so a contract can belong to a customer with no account (state `DISABLED`).
- **Turning a plan off** removes it from its product and keeps the group and selling plan in Shopify, so contracts that reference them are untouched. Nothing is deleted. Turning it on puts it back.
- **Editing:** the billing mode, releases per term and term length can't change once a plan exists, because they're what subscribers bought. Name, display name, price, renewal rule, start rule, auto-renew, giftable and product can. A new price applies to new sign-ups; Shopify's contracts keep their own price (checked live in Step 9), and the merchant chooses per change what happens to them (see "Renewals and charges").
- **Sync runs on the queue** (`plan.sync`, job ID `plan.sync:{planId}:{revision}`), reading the plan's current state, not a snapshot. Write order: lease the plan in D1, find the group (stored ID, else by code), create or update it in Shopify, store the group and selling plan IDs in D1 at once, fix the attached products, read the group back and compare, then write the status unless the plan was edited meanwhile. Statuses: not synced, syncing, in sync, differs from Shopify, error. Shopify's search by `merchant_code` is loose and needs the value quoted when it has colons; results are matched exactly.
- **Scopes** (set in `shopify.app.toml` and `SCOPES`): `write_products`, `read_customers`, `write_customers`, `read_customer_payment_methods`, `write_own_subscription_contracts`, `write_orders`, `write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders`, `write_validations` (added in Step 7 for the cart rule below), `read_shopify_payments_disputes` (added in Step 9 for chargebacks). The `read_` forms are implied by `write_`. `read_all_orders` is left for Step 14, which needs orders older than 60 days.
- **`app/scopes_update` webhook:** it used to write back the whole session it loaded, which could undo a refresh that landed in between. It now changes only the stored scope, with a compare-and-set (`updateScope`).
- **Countries:** `allowedCountries` is checked against Shopify's `CountryCode` list (243 codes, `app/countries.ts`). It isn't enforced at checkout yet.

### Subscriptions in (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 7 that Steps 8, 9, 12 and 14 live with. Checked with real test checkouts on `newstand-dev`.

- **`releasesRemaining` is the number of paid releases whose order hasn't been created.** It starts at `releasesPerTerm` for a bundle and 1 for a per-release plan, because the sign-up charge pays for the first release. It goes down by exactly 1 in the same batch that stores `Delivery.orderGid` (`createDeliveryOrder`), and at no other time: not at sign-up, not when a Delivery is assigned, not when a release ships. It counts releases, not copies. So 0 means every paid release has its order, which is what the late hold asks. A cancelled release never reaches an order, so it never counts.
- **Start rules** are evaluated at the contract's `createdAt`, so a webhook handled late gets the answer the customer signed up under. Status says whether a step has happened; a date only says whether it had happened by then, and a release with no date counts as early. Cancelled releases never count. "Current" is the latest release whose pre-order had opened at sign-up.
  - `PREORDER_SWITCHOVER`: the current release, shipped now if it's already out.
  - `CURRENT_IF_BEFORE_CUTOFF`: the current release if it wasn't yet ready to ship at sign-up; otherwise the next.
  - `NEXT`: the first release whose pre-order opens after sign-up.
  - With no release to give yet (none at all, only cancelled, or only unannounced), the subscription has no Delivery and waits. `Subscription.firstReleaseNumber` is the lowest release number it can receive, so Step 8 can tell which runs it joins. `nextReleaseId` is unused: a `PENDING` Delivery says what's next.
- **Order step:** `orderStepReached(status, orderTiming)` says whether a release's orders are due. If it's true when a sign-up is handled, the app creates that subscriber's order at once, which covers a sign-up during a pre-order under `PREORDER_OPENS` after the run has finished. Otherwise the Delivery stays `PENDING` for the run. The check runs after the Delivery is written, and the run must flip the status before it lists subscribers, so one side always sees the other.
- **$0 orders can't dedupe in Shopify.** Checked live: `orderCreate` accepts `@idempotent` and ignores it (the same key made two orders), and a metafield definition with unique values doesn't stop a second order either (it made one and silently dropped the value). So `createDeliveryOrder` takes a 5-minute lease on the Delivery, searches `source_identifier` for a marker (`release-subscriptions:delivery:<id>`), creates the order with that marker, then stores it. The search index lagged about 5 seconds when measured, and a crash leaves the lease to lapse, so the retry finds the order. Shopify's search is eventually consistent and promises no limit on that delay, so a duplicate needs an outage of the index lasting past the lease; two matches log `delivery.duplicate_orders`.
- **$0 order settings:** `sendReceipt` and `sendFulfillmentReceipt` are false, so Shopify sends nothing when the order is created. They don't affect a later shipment email, and the order gets its email address from the associated customer (both checked live). That email depends on the "Notify customer" toggle a merchant sees when fulfilling, which the app can't set: on, the timeline shows the shipping confirmation sent; off, nothing is sent. It was off at first on both $0 and checkout orders, and Shopify then remembers the last choice. `inventoryBehaviour` is `DECREMENT_IGNORING_POLICY`, because a paid release must not fail for being out of stock: checked live, it oversells and Shopify flags the order. `requiresShipping` comes from the variant, because Shopify otherwise marks the line as not shipping. No shipping or tax lines, and tax is 0 on a $0 line.
- **Orders that can't be created stay visible.** With no product, the Delivery stays `PENDING` and the releases screen shows who's waiting. A refusal from Shopify (or a contract with no address, such as pickup) is kept in `Delivery.orderError` and shown too. Choosing a product, even the same one, queues them again. "Too many attempts" isn't a refusal, though it arrives as a `userError` with no code: it throws and the job retries. Dev stores allow 5 new orders a minute.
- **`Release.productGid` holds a variant ID.** The picker is the product picker, because the variant picker shows a one-variant product with no name.
- **Payment orders:** `fulfillmentOrderClose` is for fulfillment services, so "mark fulfilled" is `fulfillmentCreate` with `notifyCustomer: false`, on only the lines belonging to a contract, so another product in the same checkout still ships. It refuses a $0 order by its marker. A fulfillment order assigned to a fulfillment service isn't visible with `write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders`, and since API version 2024-10 only that service can fulfill it, so no scope fixes this: the subscription product must stock at a merchant-managed location. The job logs `payment_order.not_visible`. Measured on three checkouts, the mark lands 17 to 18 seconds after the order is created.
- **Quantity above 1:** one Subscription with `quantity`; each $0 order ships that many copies. The contract already bills for them all, so nothing is lost and renewals stay right.
- **Several lines of one plan** add up. **A turned-off plan** still gets its subscription, because the customer has paid. **A contract with no plan of this shop's** is ignored.
- **Two plans on one contract (decided by N, 2026-11-03).** Shopify merges plans with the same billing cycle onto one contract (seen live: Yearly and Pay per issue), and a billing attempt charges a whole contract. So two things apply. A cart rule, the `one-plan-per-cart` validation Function, stops two different selling plans in a cart: checked live, `/cart/add.js` answers 422 with its message. It's turned on by the plan sync and left alone after that, so a merchant can turn it off in Shopify's checkout rules, and it lets checkouts through if it fails at runtime. It counts every app's selling plans, because a function's input doesn't say whose they are. And if a mixed contract gets through anyway, it gets no Subscription, its order stays unfulfilled, and the releases screen flags it until the merchant marks it handled.
- **Missed webhooks:** a tick finder enqueues one `contracts.reconcile` job per installed shop per hour, which reads the newest contracts and enqueues any with no Subscription, under a job ID of its own. Running twice is safe, because the job reads the contract fresh. Shopify stops retrying after a few hours, and a contract lost that way is a customer who paid and gets nothing.
- **Cake Zine's setup (from N, 2026-11-03).** On Shopify Basic. No fulfillment app or 3PL: orders are fulfilled by hand in the Shopify admin, so nothing races the payment order's mark. Shipping is included in the plan price, through a free rate on the subscription product's shipping profile; product, shipping and handling costs get recorded separately for profit reporting (Step 13). The "every year" wording at checkout stays for now, with the terms text in the plan picker explaining the real schedule; Step 17 looks for something better. Which app holds the contracts is held until Step 16.
- **GDPR:** no names, addresses or emails are stored, only Shopify IDs, statuses, dates and a gift message. `customers/data_request` writes that as a `gdpr.data_request` Event for the merchant to hand over, with no screen yet. `customers/redact` sets `customerGid` to `redacted`, clears gift fields and address confirmations, ends the subscription, cancels unfulfilled Deliveries and deletes its Events; it keeps the contract ID so a replay can't recreate the subscription, and keeps order IDs. `shop/redact` deletes every row in one batch, children first: Event, BillingAttempt, Delivery, Subscription, PriceChange, Plan, Release, Shop, plus JobRun and OfflineSession. These jobs use Shopify's webhook ID and carry no `shop`, so `JobRun` keeps neither.

### Release runs (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 8 that Steps 9, 10, 13, 14 and 15 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` with real contracts.

- **A run is state-driven.** A release at its order step with subscribers due and no order is work to do, whoever noticed it. Each hour `findReleaseRuns` gives every open release at its step a `release.run` job, whose ID has the hour in it, so a `DEAD` or finished job from an earlier hour never blocks it. That recovers a crash after the status flip, a lost message, a chain that died, a late subscriber and a change of `orderTiming`, all within an hour.
- **A run is a chain of slices, not one job per order.** `runReleaseSlice` assigns who's due, makes up to 20 orders, and returns the next slice. `Release.runLeaseUntil` keeps it to one chain per release, and covers a slice's sleep, so a tick arriving during a throttled wait stands aside. `delivery.order` stays for one sign-up's first order: if it goes `DEAD`, the run picks that Delivery up, because it reads what's `PENDING` and not what jobs exist.
- **Throttling isn't failure.** On "Too many attempts" the slice finishes `DONE` and enqueues the next with a delay, so a limit never uses up the queue's retries. It waits 60 seconds, doubling to 15 minutes only while nothing gets through, with no assumed rate. Dev and trial stores allow 5 new orders a minute (documented). Shopify publishes no figure for paid plans: its staff say the standard API rates apply and it isn't throttled, so treat that as likely, not promised, and watch `release_run.throttled` on Cake Zine's first real run.
- **What's left after a pass** (a held lease or an unexpected error) gets up to 3 more passes, 6 minutes apart, past an order's 5-minute lease, and then waits for the tick. One bad order never holds up the rest. A refusal (`Delivery.orderError`) is left out of runs until the merchant chooses a product again, which clears it.
- **Assigning is one `INSERT ... SELECT`** (`createDueDeliveries`), not batches of rows: it's atomic, needs no read first, and takes 1,000 rows in one statement. It reads the release's status itself, so nothing is assigned before the step is reached, and the status flip always comes first.
- **Paid count:** every `PENDING` Delivery has a claim on one of `releasesRemaining`, including a refused one or one waiting for a product, and a subscriber joins a run only with a paid release left over. So `PENDING` Deliveries never outnumber `releasesRemaining`, and two runs at once can't spend one release twice. `createDeliveryOrder` also returns `no_balance` rather than create an order with nothing paid, and the count stops at 0. Whoever is held up on an earlier release stays visible there, and cancelling that release frees them for the next run.
- **`PAUSED` is included:** it only spends releases already paid for, and `docs/PLAN.md` says a paused subscriber's unpaid release ships late once paid. The exception is a chargeback, which holds paid releases too (see "Renewals and charges").
- **A shipped release is closed:** no new Deliveries, though it finishes the ones it holds. `moveRelease` assigns anyone still due just before the flip, so marking as shipped right after a crash loses no one. A subscriber who misses a closed release keeps their paid balance for the next one, so nothing is lost, and a later renewal can't reach back to it.
- **Cancel is refused once any order exists for the release, or any order lease is held** (a lease left by a crash may mean an order Shopify has and D1 doesn't), checked in the same statement as the status change. Otherwise it cancels the `PENDING` Deliveries in one batch, and those subscribers join the next run with their balance untouched. `createDeliveryOrder` only leases a `PENDING` Delivery, so a cancel racing a job has one winner. Under `PREORDER_OPENS` this means a pre-order can't be cancelled after its first order. Marking those orders cancelled in Shopify doesn't change that, and the merchant still has to ship or refund them.
- **Dates:** before a step happens, `preorderOpensAt` and `cutoffAt` are the plan of a `SCHEDULED` release. When it happens, by button or by tick, `transitionRelease` overwrites the date with the real moment. That keeps "status says whether, the date says when" true, so a webhook handled late gets the answer a prompt one got even when the tick fired late. A date being set must be in the future. A date left unchanged can have passed, and the tick fires it, or both in order. Going back to `MANUAL` clears the plan. A ready date on an unannounced release needs a pre-order date. Times are whole hours, because the tick is hourly.
- **Timezone:** `Shop.timezone` holds Shopify's `ianaTimezone`, read on the first page load and again each time dates are saved, so a page load needs no Admin API call. Dates are stored in UTC, and already-stored ones don't change if the store moves timezone.
- **CSV:** one D1 query and no Shopify call, so it stays fast and no names or addresses enter D1 or pass through. Columns: release, order number, order link, status, problem, quantity, plan, customer link, contract ID. For labels or addresses, each order has a `release-<number>` tag to filter on in Shopify, which has its own export. `Delivery.orderName` was added for this, and orders from before this step show their ID instead. Spreadsheet formulas get a leading apostrophe.
- **Contract webhooks are keyed on Shopify's webhook ID, not `revision_id`.** Found live: cancelling a contract leaves `revisionId` unchanged, so `/update`'s job had the same ID as `/create`'s and was skipped as a repeat, and the subscription stayed `ACTIVE`. A cancellation whose webhook never arrives isn't recovered by the hourly check, which only looks for contracts with no Subscription, so the contract's status is read before every charge.
- **Charging in a run:** Step 9 added a second way in, for a pay-per-release subscriber at 0 (see "Renewals and charges"). `createDeliveryOrder` still refuses an order with nothing paid.

### Renewals and charges (2026-11-04)

Technical choices from Step 9 that Steps 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` with the test gateway.

**What Shopify does (checked live):**

- **Billing dates:** a `subscriptionBillingAttemptCreate` with no selector is accepted on any date, any number of times in a cycle, and leaves `nextBillingDate` alone, so that date is only a label. Left out, `originTime` is the moment of the call and the billing order's fulfillment order is `OPEN` and can be fulfilled at once. Set in the future, it's `SCHEDULED` with only `MARK_AS_OPEN` allowed, which would block the payment order's mark, so the app never sets it.
- **Idempotency:** a key Shopify has seen returns the existing attempt, whatever state it's in, and charges nothing. That's the base of exactly-once. A result took about 2 seconds, and its webhook was settled within about 10.
- **Limits:** five attempts in about 7 seconds on one contract were fine, and the sixth was refused with `Billing rate limit exceeded for this contract` (a `THROTTLED` GraphQL error). Billing orders don't appear to count towards the dev store's limit of 5 new orders a minute: `orderCreate` still worked right after five billing orders in a minute.
- **The billing order** has `sourceName` `subscription_contract_checkout_one`, and its lines carry `contract`, so `markPaymentOrderFulfilled` works on it unchanged, about 16 seconds after creation, with no shipping email on the timeline.
- **`deliveryPrice`:** a charge bills each line's `currentPrice` times its quantity, plus the contract's own `deliveryPrice`. That comes from the rate chosen at checkout and stays on the contract: a contract with $8 billed $8 again, and one with $0 billed $0 (seen on order #1093, $80 with $0 shipping). So a free rate at checkout is what makes renewals free, and the app adds and changes nothing. Cake Zine's free rate (Step 16) must be in place before its first sign-up, because a contract that starts with $8 keeps it until someone changes it with a contract draft.
- **Customer email:** Shopify sends its order confirmation for every billing order. The billing attempt has no option for this, unlike `orderCreate`'s `sendReceipt`, and it can't be switched off per merchant from the app: a merchant can only edit the template, and from what we could find it can't be turned off on ordinary plans. A failed attempt left nothing on the customer's timeline. Accepted by N as an exception to "each email switchable" (decided by N, 2026-11-04). Step 10's emails should be written knowing the customer also gets this one.
- **Failing on purpose:** the test gateway only vaults a card that succeeds. `customerPaymentMethodRevoke` needs a scope the app doesn't have, and a contract's card can't be set to null. What does fail for real is a contract with no card (`PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SPECIFIED`), which a draft's `paymentMethodId` can then fix. A real decline has only been tested against a fake.

**How a charge works:**

- **Key:** `subscriptionId:chargeFor:attempt` (`BillingAttempt.chargeFor`). For a pay-per-release charge, `chargeFor` is the release ID, as `AGENTS.md` has it. A bundle renewal has no release, so it's `term-<termEndsAt in ms>`, which also means a term can only renew once, whichever way it was triggered.
- **Exactly once:** the row goes in before Shopify is called, the same key goes out on any repeat, and only `retryDue` or `retryCharge` can ask for a new attempt number, one more than a row that has failed. Success is one batch whose every statement checks the attempt isn't yet marked `SUCCEEDED`, and marking it comes last.
- **Results:** the webhook route only enqueues `billing.settle`, which reads the attempt fresh from Shopify and finds its row by key. So duplicates and order don't matter, and it can't arrive before the row. The tick looks up any row still `PENDING` after 10 minutes (`billing.recover`). Without a Shopify ID, it searches the contract for the key, and only if it isn't there re-sends the key, unless the subscriber can no longer be charged, when the row is deleted. A success after a failure counts: it's how a card a bank challenged (`ACTION_REQUIRED`) comes back.
- **In a run:** charges come after the slice's orders, and only as many as the run could order now, minus those still waiting for a result, at most 20. So on a slow store a charge waits minutes for its order rather than hours, and a throttled order can't re-charge because the run charges on a balance of 0. The webhook's success starts a slice, which makes the order. No product means no charge.
- **Who's in:** `DUE_SUBSCRIPTION` in `app/release-runs.server.ts`. A pay-per-release subscriber who is `ACTIVE`, at 0, with no `PENDING` Delivery and auto-renew on gets a `PENDING` Delivery and a charge. One `PENDING` at a time, so a charge is spent on the release it was for. A paid first release isn't charged. A gift never is.
- **Before every charge:** the contract is read, because a lost cancellation isn't otherwise recovered. `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` cancels the subscription and closes what's held or unpaid, with no charge. Anything else not `ACTIVE` charges nothing and changes nothing. `inventoryPolicy` is `ALLOW_OVERSELLING`, because the subscription item isn't what ships.

**Renewal rules:** one function, `renewalDue`, on `ACTIVE`, auto-renew, not a gift and no payment problem. `TERM_DATE_HOLD_IF_LATE`: term date passed and balance 0, so a held one is charged right after the last paid order is created. `TERM_DATE`: term date passed, whatever the balance. `ON_DEPLETION`: balance 0, whatever the date, and a bundle of one can't choose it, because a first release shipped at once would charge a second time within a minute. Both the order and the tick trigger them, and the tick catches a crash in between.

**How a new term is measured (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** from the later of the old term's end and when the charge landed, plus the plan's months. So a held or late renewal buys a whole term, and the next charge can't come sooner than a term after this one. Counting from the old date would make a renewal held for 14 months on a 12-month term due again at once under `TERM_DATE`. A renewal on time drifts by up to an hour a year, from the hourly tick.

**Reach-back, on a bundle:** when a renewal first comes due with a balance of 0, `firstReleaseNumber` moves past every release that has reached its order step. Those are extra and stay out, however long they stay open. A release reached after that point while the payment is failing is a `HELD` Delivery, tied to that release, which ignores `firstReleaseNumber` and ships late once paid. A renewal with a balance left moves nothing, because paid releases are still owed. On recovery the oldest `HELD` Deliveries go as far as the balance covers, and the others are cancelled, since a bundle buys N releases.

**A missed payment, on pay per release (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** one missed payment holds back later releases. When the charge for Issue 5 fails, Issue 5's Delivery is `HELD`. From that first failure, through the retry days and the pause, Issues 6 and 7 get no charge and no Delivery. Recovery is one charge, for Issue 5, which then ships late. Whichever way the problem ends, one batch clears it and moves `firstReleaseNumber` past every release that has reached its order step, so 6 and 7 are skipped for good, even one still open, and the hourly scan can't assign it. That includes a recovery where Issue 5 itself was cancelled, which charges nothing. Issue 8 onward is charged and shipped as normal. A paid balance still ships meanwhile, as it does for anyone paused.

**Failed cards:**

- **Retries:** three tries, on days 0, 3 and 7, then `PAUSED` with `pausedReason` `FAILED_PAYMENT`. `NOT_WORTH_RETRYING` in `app/billing.server.ts` pauses at once for expired or invalid cards, fraud, a bank's challenge and the like. Unknown codes are retried, because gateways often report soft declines generically. A missing card is retried too, since it never reaches a card network. The public "pause or cancel after retries" setting waits for Step 17 (decided by N, 2026-11-04), because it needs a refund decision and a settings screen, so everyone pauses for now.
- **Held:** `Subscription.paymentProblemSince` is set from the first failure, not just the pause. On a bundle, that makes a release reached during retries `HELD`, so it can't be missed by marking it shipped. On pay per release, only the release whose charge failed is `HELD`. `HELD` doesn't count as refused, shows on the releases screen and is in the CSV. Cancelling a release cancels its `HELD` Deliveries, and then nothing is owed for them.
- **Recovery:** `retryCharge`, through the `billing.resume` job. Today the merchant presses Retry charge in Payment problems on the releases screen. **Step 11 calls the same job when a customer updates their card.** A contract with no card also recovers by itself on its next scheduled retry.

**Chargeback (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** pause and flag, and it holds paid releases too, until the merchant ends it: the customer is disputing what they paid, so shipping more only adds to the loss. This differs from a failed card, which still ships what's paid. `disputes/create` (scope `read_shopify_payments_disputes`, chargebacks only, not inquiries) finds the contract through the order's lines, so it covers the sign-up charge too. `PENDING` Deliveries become `HELD`, new ones are `HELD`, retries stop, and it stays flagged through a cancellation. Only the merchant ends it, with the same button, whichever way the dispute went. On pay per release, ending it lets go what the balance covers, then the oldest `HELD` Delivery left is charged once and the rest are closed, so no second charge follows. Ending it on a cancelled subscription keeps it `CANCELLED` and charges nothing: what's held goes as far as the balance covers, and the rest is closed. A release whose Delivery the cancellation already closed isn't offered again, so that balance ships on the next release, as any cancelled subscriber's does.

**Price changes:** the merchant chooses per change on the plan form, and `updatePlan` records a `PriceChange`. New sign-ups always get the new price, through the selling plan sync. `APPLY_AT_RENEWAL` has an `effectiveAt` 30 days out, for the notice, which is Step 10's and uses `noticeSentAt`. Who it reaches: subscribers on the plan who signed up before `effectiveAt` and whose `lockedPriceCents` differs. Just before a charge's first attempt, once `effectiveAt` has passed, the app updates the contract with a draft (`subscriptionDraftLineUpdate`, per copy, so quantity above 1 stays right) and then `lockedPriceCents`. A retry never does this, so a price change can't apply to a charge already attempted. Only the plan's latest change counts, so a merchant can take one back by saving another with `KEEP_OLD_PRICE`. The 30-day notice is decided by N, 2026-11-04. **Step 10 must send that notice: nothing does yet.**

**Keeping tokens alive:** no separate job. `contracts.reconcile` runs hourly for every shop with a session, and its `unauthenticated.admin` goes through `loadSession`, which refreshes an access token within 5 minutes of expiry. Access tokens last about an hour, so a refresh happens about hourly, and each one starts the 90-day refresh token's clock again (read from D1: `refreshTokenExpires` was about 90 days out). A shop idle for 80 days, or any number of days, is refreshed by the next tick. A dead token (a stopped Worker, say, for over 90 days) logs `session.refresh_rejected` at error level, and every job for the shop fails and goes `DEAD`. Nothing is lost, because all of this is state-driven and job IDs carry the hour: runs, renewals and retries are found again each tick, and the merchant's next page load gets a new token by exchange. The merchant isn't told, though, since the app has no way to reach them until Step 10.

**Events:** `billing.succeeded`, `billing.failed` (`nextRetryAt` null means it paused) and `billing.chargeback`, for Step 10's emails and Flow triggers. A late-renewal notice can find its people from `termEndsAt` passed and a balance above 0. Nothing writes a row for that yet.
