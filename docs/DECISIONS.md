### Offline tokens moved from KV to D1 (2026-11-03)

Background jobs depend on Shopify's expiring offline tokens, so Step 3 tested them on `newstand-dev`.

- **Safe:** two refreshes at once, or a refresh from a stale read. The old refresh token keeps working until its successor is used, and it returns the same successor.
- **Safe, as far as tested:** two token exchanges at once. In 2 of 2 rounds on a cold token they returned the same token, and in 3 more on a fresh one both results still refreshed. Both used one ID token, because App Bridge returned the same one. Shopify doesn't document this, so it could change. It's why nothing stops the admin's parallel loaders from each running an exchange on first load.
- **Not safe:** a refresh racing a token exchange. In 2 of 3 rounds both returned 200 but the refresh's new refresh token was already dead, and in the third the refresh itself got a 401. If the dead token is stored last, jobs fail once the access token expires, until the merchant next opens the app. An exchange on a token that has gone cold also retires the previous refresh token straight away.

The library ran an exchange whenever an admin request found a token within 5 minutes of expiry, and a job refreshing at that moment could hit this race. So `app/session-storage.server.ts` now refreshes on load, for admin requests and jobs alike, and an exchange only happens when there's no session or Shopify rejects the refresh token. This departs from Shopify's advice to use a token exchange during an active merchant session, because that advice is what makes the race possible.

Sessions moved to D1 because the KV adapter doesn't fit refreshing: its per-shop list grows on every store and is read key by key on uninstall, and KV limits writes to one per second per key, which two parallel loaders can exceed. D1 has neither problem and no stale reads. The cost is one small D1 read per `authenticate.admin` call.

### Plans and selling plans (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 5 that Steps 6, 7 and 9 live with.

- **One selling plan group per plan, holding one selling plan**, attached to one product. The group's `merchantCode` is the plan's ID, which is how a sync finds a group whose ID never reached D1. Shopify's admin shows that code as the group's title on the product (cosmetic).
- **Customers see `displayName`** as both the group and the selling plan name; the group's option is named "Subscription".
- **Price:** a fixed `PRICE` pricing policy, so the plan's price replaces the product's at checkout. The selling plan's currency is the store's, read at sync time and stored on the plan.
- **Schedule:** Shopify never bills a contract by itself, only a `subscriptionBillingAttemptCreate` from the app does, and the delivery policy only labels the contract. So the intervals are chosen to be harmless: a bundle uses its term (`YEAR` x years if the term is whole years, else `MONTH` x months), so the contract's next billing date is the term date; a per-release plan uses `MONTH` x 12 (it was `YEAR` x 1 until N changed it in Step 16, so it can't share a contract with a 12-month bundle: see "Cutover and install"). No anchors, cutoff, minimum or maximum cycles. The delivery policy is `FULFILLMENT_BEGIN` with `ASAP`. **Verified live in Step 6 (two test checkouts):** both contracts read back `billingPolicy` and `deliveryPolicy` as `YEAR` x 1 with no anchors or cycle limits, and `nextBillingDate` is one year after checkout, at 16:00 UTC (11:00 in the store's New York time) rather than the checkout time. Step 9 confirmed a billing attempt outside the cycle date is accepted (see "Renewals and charges").
- **Group `appId`:** every group carries `appId: "release-subscriptions"` (`SELLING_PLAN_APP_ID`). Liquid exposes it as `selling_plan_group.app_id`, which is how the theme block shows only this app's plans; it was `null` before, so there was no other way to tell the app's groups apart. A sync puts it on groups made earlier.
- **One-time purchases:** while a product has a plan attached, the sync sets `requiresSellingPlan` on it, so Shopify refuses a cart without a plan (checked: add to cart and a `/cart/<variant>:1` link both fail). When a plan leaves a product that now has no selling plan group at all, the sync sets it back to false, because a product that requires a plan and has none can't be bought. A product that still has any group (this app's or another's) keeps it.
- **Shippable check:** the sync reports MISMATCH when a variant of the product isn't a physical product (`requiresShipping` false), because checkout then collects no shipping address (checked live: only a billing address was asked). The app doesn't change the variant.
- **Contract facts (Step 6, test gateway, guest checkout):** the contract is `ACTIVE`, its line's `sellingPlanId` is the plan's selling plan, and its `originOrder` is the checkout order: `PAID`, `UNFULFILLED`, with a `SHIPPING` fulfillment order `OPEN` at the shop location (the app must mark it fulfilled in Step 7). The order's line item has the plan too. The store's customer accounts setting is optional, and checkout worked as a guest, so a contract can belong to a customer with no account (state `DISABLED`).
- **Turning a plan off** removes it from its product and keeps the group and selling plan in Shopify, so contracts that reference them are untouched. Nothing is deleted. Turning it on puts it back.
- **Editing:** the billing mode, releases per term and term length can't change once a plan exists, because they're what subscribers bought. Name, display name, price, renewal rule, start rule, auto-renew, giftable and product can. A new price applies to new sign-ups; Shopify's contracts keep their own price (checked live in Step 9), and the merchant chooses per change what happens to them (see "Renewals and charges").
- **Sync runs on the queue** (`plan.sync`, job ID `plan.sync:{planId}:{revision}`), reading the plan's current state, not a snapshot. Write order: lease the plan in D1, find the group (stored ID, else by code), create or update it in Shopify, store the group and selling plan IDs in D1 at once, fix the attached products, read the group back and compare, then write the status unless the plan was edited meanwhile. Statuses: not synced, syncing, in sync, differs from Shopify, error. Shopify's search by `merchant_code` is loose and needs the value quoted when it has colons; results are matched exactly.
- **Scopes** (set in `shopify.app.toml` and `SCOPES`): `write_products`, `read_customers`, `write_customers`, `read_customer_payment_methods`, `write_own_subscription_contracts`, `write_orders`, `write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders`, `write_validations` (added in Step 7 for the cart rule below), `read_shopify_payments_disputes` (added in Step 9 for chargebacks). The `read_` forms are implied by `write_`. `read_all_orders` was added in Step 14, which needs orders older than 60 days.
- **`app/scopes_update` webhook:** it used to write back the whole session it loaded, which could undo a refresh that landed in between. It now changes only the stored scope, with a compare-and-set (`updateScope`).
- **Countries:** `allowedCountries` is checked against Shopify's `CountryCode` list (243 codes, `app/countries.ts`). It isn't enforced at checkout yet.

### Subscriptions in (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 7 that Steps 8, 9, 12 and 14 live with. Checked with real test checkouts on `newstand-dev`.

- **`releasesRemaining` is the number of paid releases whose order hasn't been created.** It starts at `releasesPerTerm` for a bundle and 1 for a per-release plan, because the sign-up charge pays for the first release. It goes down by exactly 1 in the same batch that stores `Delivery.orderGid` (`createDeliveryOrder`), and at no other time: not at sign-up, not when a Delivery is assigned, not when a release ships. It counts releases, not copies. So 0 means every paid release has its order, which is what the late hold asks. A cancelled release never reaches an order, so it never counts.
- **Start rules** are evaluated at the contract's `createdAt`, so a webhook handled late gets the answer the customer signed up under. Status says whether a step has happened; a date only says whether it had happened by then, and a release with no date counts as early. Cancelled releases never count. "Current" is the latest release whose pre-order had opened at sign-up.
  - `PREORDER_SWITCHOVER`: the current release, shipped now if it's already out.
  - `CURRENT_IF_BEFORE_CUTOFF`: the current release if it wasn't yet ready to ship at sign-up; otherwise the next.
  - `NEXT`: the first release whose pre-order opens after sign-up.
  - With no release to give yet (none at all, only cancelled, or only unannounced), the subscription has no Delivery and waits. `Subscription.firstReleaseNumber` is the lowest release number it can receive, so Step 8 can tell which runs it joins. `nextReleaseId` is unused: a `PENDING` Delivery says what's next.
- **Order step:** `orderStepReached(status, orderTiming)` says whether a release's orders are due. If it's true when a sign-up is handled, the app creates that subscriber's order at once, which covers a sign-up during a pre-order under `PREORDER_OPENS` after the run has finished. Otherwise the Delivery stays `PENDING` for the run. The check runs after the Delivery is written, and the run must flip the status before it lists subscribers, so one side always sees the other.
- **$0 orders can't dedupe in Shopify.** Checked live: `orderCreate` accepts `@idempotent` and ignores it (the same key made two orders), and a metafield definition with unique values doesn't stop a second order either (it made one and silently dropped the value). So `createDeliveryOrder` takes a 5-minute lease on the Delivery, searches `source_identifier` for a marker (`release-subscriptions:delivery:<id>`), creates the order with that marker, then stores it. The search index lagged about 5 seconds when measured, and a crash leaves the lease to lapse, so the retry finds the order. Shopify's search is eventually consistent and promises no limit on that delay, so a duplicate needs an outage of the index lasting past the lease; two matches log `delivery.duplicate_orders`.
- **$0 order settings:** `sendReceipt` and `sendFulfillmentReceipt` are false, so Shopify sends nothing when the order is created. They don't affect a later shipment email, and the order gets its email address from the associated customer (both checked live). That email depends on the "Notify customer" toggle a merchant sees when fulfilling, which the app can't set: on, the timeline shows the shipping confirmation sent; off, nothing is sent. It was off at first on both $0 and checkout orders, and Shopify then remembers the last choice. `inventoryBehaviour` is `DECREMENT_IGNORING_POLICY`, because a paid release must not fail for being out of stock: checked live, it oversells and Shopify flags the order. `requiresShipping` comes from the variant, because Shopify otherwise marks the line as not shipping. No shipping or tax lines, and tax is 0 on a $0 line.
- **Orders that can't be created stay visible.** With no product, the Delivery stays `PENDING` and the releases screen shows who's waiting. A refusal from Shopify (or a contract with no address, such as pickup) is kept in `Delivery.orderError` and shown too. Choosing a product, even the same one, queues them again. "Too many attempts" isn't a refusal, though it arrives as a `userError` with no code: it throws and the job retries. Dev stores allow 5 new orders a minute.
- **`Release.productGid` holds a variant ID.** The picker is the product picker, because the variant picker shows a one-variant product with no name.
- **Payment orders:** `fulfillmentOrderClose` is for fulfillment services, so "mark fulfilled" is `fulfillmentCreate` with `notifyCustomer: false`, on only the lines belonging to a contract, so another product in the same checkout still ships. It refuses a $0 order by its marker. A fulfillment order assigned to a fulfillment service isn't visible with `write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders`, and since API version 2024-10 only that service can fulfill it, so no scope fixes this: the subscription product must stock at a merchant-managed location. The job logs `payment_order.not_visible`. Measured on three checkouts, the mark lands 17 to 18 seconds after the order is created.
- **Quantity above 1:** one Subscription with `quantity`; each $0 order ships that many copies. The contract already bills for them all, so nothing is lost and renewals stay right.
- **Several lines of one plan** add up. **A turned-off plan** still gets its subscription, because the customer has paid. **A contract with no plan of this shop's** is ignored.
- **Two plans on one contract (decided by N, 2026-11-03).** Shopify merges plans with the same billing cycle onto one contract (seen live: Yearly and Pay per issue), and a billing attempt charges a whole contract. So two things apply. A cart rule, the `one-plan-per-cart` validation Function, stops two different selling plans in a cart: checked live, `/cart/add.js` answers 422 with its message. It's turned on by the plan sync and left alone after that, so a merchant can turn it off in Shopify's checkout rules, and it lets checkouts through if it fails at runtime. It counts every app's selling plans, because a function's input doesn't say whose they are. And if a mixed contract gets through anyway, it gets no Subscription, its order stays unfulfilled, and the releases screen flags it until the merchant marks it handled.
- **Missed webhooks:** a tick finder enqueues one `contracts.reconcile` job per installed shop per hour, which reads the newest contracts and enqueues any with no Subscription, under a job ID of its own. Running twice is safe, because the job reads the contract fresh. Shopify stops retrying after a few hours, and a contract lost that way is a customer who paid and gets nothing.
- **Cake Zine's setup (from N, 2026-11-03).** On Shopify Basic. No fulfillment app or 3PL: orders are fulfilled by hand in the Shopify admin, so nothing races the payment order's mark. Shipping is included in the plan price, through a free rate on the subscription product's shipping profile; product, shipping and handling costs get recorded separately for profit reporting (Step 13). Checkout says "every 12 months" for a per-release plan and "every year" for the bundle (N, 2026-11-05, replacing "every year" for both), with the terms text in the plan picker explaining the real schedule; Step 17 looks for something better. Which app holds the contracts was decided in Step 16 (see "Cutover and install").
- **GDPR:** no names, addresses or emails are stored, only Shopify IDs, statuses, dates and a gift message. `customers/data_request` writes that as a `gdpr.data_request` Event for the merchant to hand over, from a banner on the releases screen (see "Hardening"). `customers/redact` sets `customerGid` to `redacted`, clears gift fields and address confirmations, ends the subscription, cancels unfulfilled Deliveries and deletes its Events; it keeps the contract ID so a replay can't recreate the subscription, and keeps order IDs. `shop/redact` deletes every row in one batch, children first: Event, BillingAttempt, Delivery, Subscription, PriceChange, Plan, Release, Shop, plus JobRun and OfflineSession. These jobs use Shopify's webhook ID and carry no `shop`, so `JobRun` keeps neither.

### Release runs (2026-11-03)

Technical choices from Step 8 that Steps 9, 10, 13, 14 and 15 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` with real contracts.

- **A run is state-driven.** A release at its order step with subscribers due and no order is work to do, whoever noticed it. Each hour `findReleaseRuns` gives every open release at its step a `release.run` job, whose ID has the hour in it, so a `DEAD` or finished job from an earlier hour never blocks it. That recovers a crash after the status flip, a lost message, a chain that died, a late subscriber and a change of `orderTiming`, all within an hour.
- **A run is a chain of slices, not one job per order.** `runReleaseSlice` assigns who's due, makes up to 20 orders, and returns the next slice. `Release.runLeaseUntil` keeps it to one chain per release, and covers a slice's sleep, so a tick arriving during a throttled wait stands aside. `delivery.order` stays for one sign-up's first order: if it goes `DEAD`, the run picks that Delivery up, because it reads what's `PENDING` and not what jobs exist.
- **Throttling isn't failure.** On "Too many attempts" the slice finishes `DONE` and enqueues the next with a delay, so a limit never uses up the queue's retries. It waits 60 seconds, doubling to 15 minutes only while nothing gets through, with no assumed rate. Dev and trial stores allow 5 new orders a minute (documented). Shopify publishes no figure for paid plans: its staff say the standard API rates apply and it isn't throttled, so treat that as likely, not promised, and watch `release_run.throttled` on Cake Zine's first real run.
- **What's left after a pass** (a held lease or an unexpected error) gets up to 3 more passes, 6 minutes apart, past an order's 5-minute lease, and then waits for the tick. One bad order never holds up the rest. A refusal (`Delivery.orderError`) is left out of runs until the merchant chooses a product again, which clears it.
- **Assigning is one `INSERT ... SELECT`** (`createDueDeliveries`), not batches of rows: it's atomic, needs no read first, and takes 1,000 rows in one statement. It reads the release's status itself, so nothing is assigned before the step is reached, and the status flip always comes first.
- **Paid count:** every `PENDING` Delivery has a claim on one of `releasesRemaining`, including a refused one or one waiting for a product, and a subscriber joins a run only with a paid release left over. So `PENDING` Deliveries never outnumber `releasesRemaining`, and two runs at once can't spend one release twice. `createDeliveryOrder` also returns `no_balance` rather than create an order with nothing paid, and the count stops at 0. Whoever is held up on an earlier release stays visible there, and cancelling that release frees them for the next run.
- **`PAUSED` is included:** it only spends releases already paid for, and `docs/PLAN.md` says a paused subscriber's unpaid release ships late once paid. The exception is a chargeback, which holds paid releases too (see "Renewals and charges").
- **A shipped release is closed:** no new Deliveries, though it finishes the ones it holds. `moveRelease` assigns anyone still due just before the flip, so marking as shipped right after a crash loses no one. A subscriber who misses a closed release keeps their paid balance for the next one, so nothing is lost, and a later renewal can't reach back to it.
- **Cancel is refused once any order exists for the release, or any order lease is held** (a lease left by a crash may mean an order Shopify has and D1 doesn't), checked in the same statement as the status change. Otherwise it cancels the `PENDING` Deliveries in one batch, and those subscribers join the next run with their balance untouched. `createDeliveryOrder` only leases a `PENDING` Delivery, so a cancel racing a job has one winner. Under `PREORDER_OPENS` this means a pre-order can't be cancelled after its first order. Marking those orders cancelled in Shopify doesn't change that, and the merchant still has to ship or refund them.
- **Dates:** before a step happens, `preorderOpensAt` and `cutoffAt` are the plan of a `SCHEDULED` release. When it happens, by button or by tick, `transitionRelease` overwrites the date with the real moment. That keeps "status says whether, the date says when" true, so a webhook handled late gets the answer a prompt one got even when the tick fired late. A date being set must be in the future. A date left unchanged can have passed, and the tick fires it, or both in order. Going back to `MANUAL` clears the plan. A ready date on an unannounced release needs a pre-order date. Times are whole hours, because the tick is hourly.
- **Timezone:** `Shop.timezone` holds Shopify's `ianaTimezone`, read on the first page load and again each time dates are saved, so a page load needs no Admin API call. Dates are stored in UTC, and already-stored ones don't change if the store moves timezone.
- **CSV:** one D1 query and no Shopify call, so it stays fast and no names or addresses enter D1 or pass through. Columns: release, order number, order link, status, problem, quantity, plan, customer link, contract ID. For labels or addresses, each order has a `release-<number>` tag to filter on in Shopify, which has its own export. `Delivery.orderName` was added for this, and orders from before this step show their ID instead. Spreadsheet formulas get a leading apostrophe.
- **Contract webhooks are keyed on Shopify's webhook ID, not `revision_id`.** Found live: cancelling a contract leaves `revisionId` unchanged, so `/update`'s job had the same ID as `/create`'s and was skipped as a repeat, and the subscription stayed `ACTIVE`. A cancellation whose webhook never arrives isn't recovered by the hourly check, which only looks for contracts with no Subscription, so the contract's status is read before every charge.
- **Charging in a run:** Step 9 added a second way in, for a pay-per-release subscriber at 0 (see "Renewals and charges"). `createDeliveryOrder` still refuses an order with nothing paid.

### Renewals and charges (2026-11-04)

Technical choices from Step 9 that Steps 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` with the test gateway.

**What Shopify does (checked live):**

- **Billing dates:** a `subscriptionBillingAttemptCreate` with no selector is accepted on any date, any number of times in a cycle, and leaves `nextBillingDate` alone, so that date is only a label. Left out, `originTime` is the moment of the call and the billing order's fulfillment order is `OPEN` and can be fulfilled at once. Set in the future, it's `SCHEDULED` with only `MARK_AS_OPEN` allowed, which would block the payment order's mark, so the app never sets it.
- **Idempotency:** a key Shopify has seen returns the existing attempt, whatever state it's in, and charges nothing. That's the base of exactly-once. A result took about 2 seconds, and its webhook was settled within about 10.
- **Limits:** five attempts in about 7 seconds on one contract were fine, and the sixth was refused with `Billing rate limit exceeded for this contract` (a `THROTTLED` GraphQL error). Billing orders don't appear to count towards the dev store's limit of 5 new orders a minute: `orderCreate` still worked right after five billing orders in a minute.
- **The billing order** has `sourceName` `subscription_contract_checkout_one`, and its lines carry `contract`, so `markPaymentOrderFulfilled` works on it unchanged, about 16 seconds after creation, with no shipping email on the timeline.
- **`deliveryPrice`:** a charge bills each line's `currentPrice` times its quantity, plus the contract's own `deliveryPrice`. That comes from the rate chosen at checkout and stays on the contract: a contract with $8 billed $8 again, and one with $0 billed $0 (seen on order #1093, $80 with $0 shipping). So a free rate at checkout is what makes renewals free, and the app adds and changes nothing. Cake Zine's free rate (Step 16) must be in place before its first sign-up, because a contract that starts with $8 keeps it until someone changes it with a contract draft.
- **Customer email:** Shopify sends its order confirmation for every billing order. The billing attempt has no option for this, unlike `orderCreate`'s `sendReceipt`, and it can't be switched off per merchant from the app: a merchant can only edit the template, and from what we could find it can't be turned off on ordinary plans. A failed attempt left nothing on the customer's timeline. Accepted by N as an exception to "each email switchable" (decided by N, 2026-11-04). Step 10's emails should be written knowing the customer also gets this one.
- **Failing on purpose:** the test gateway only vaults a card that succeeds. `customerPaymentMethodRevoke` needs a scope the app doesn't have, and a contract's card can't be set to null. What does fail for real is a contract with no card (`PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SPECIFIED`), which a draft's `paymentMethodId` can then fix. A real decline has only been tested against a fake.

**How a charge works:**

- **Key:** `subscriptionId:chargeFor:attempt` (`BillingAttempt.chargeFor`). For a pay-per-release charge, `chargeFor` is the release ID, as `AGENTS.md` has it. A bundle renewal has no release, so it's `term-<termEndsAt in ms>`, which also means a term can only renew once, whichever way it was triggered.
- **Exactly once:** the row goes in before Shopify is called, the same key goes out on any repeat, and only `retryDue` or `retryCharge` can ask for a new attempt number, one more than a row that has failed. Success is one batch whose every statement checks the attempt isn't yet marked `SUCCEEDED`, and marking it comes last.
- **Results:** the webhook route only enqueues `billing.settle`, which reads the attempt fresh from Shopify and finds its row by key. So duplicates and order don't matter, and it can't arrive before the row. The tick looks up any row still `PENDING` after 10 minutes (`billing.recover`). Without a Shopify ID, it searches the contract for the key, and only if it isn't there re-sends the key, unless the subscriber can no longer be charged, when the row is deleted. A success after a failure counts: it's how a card a bank challenged (`ACTION_REQUIRED`) comes back.
- **In a run:** charges come after the slice's orders, and only as many as the run could order now, minus those still waiting for a result, at most 20. So on a slow store a charge waits minutes for its order rather than hours, and a throttled order can't re-charge because the run charges on a balance of 0. The webhook's success starts a slice, which makes the order. No product means no charge.
- **Who's in:** `DUE_SUBSCRIPTION` in `app/release-runs.server.ts`. A pay-per-release subscriber who is `ACTIVE`, at 0, with no `PENDING` Delivery and auto-renew on gets a `PENDING` Delivery and a charge. One `PENDING` at a time, so a charge is spent on the release it was for. A paid first release isn't charged. A gift never is.
- **Before every charge:** the contract is read, because a lost cancellation isn't otherwise recovered. `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` cancels the subscription and closes what's held or unpaid, with no charge. Anything else not `ACTIVE` charges nothing and changes nothing. `inventoryPolicy` is `ALLOW_OVERSELLING`, because the subscription item isn't what ships.

**Renewal rules:** one function, `renewalDue`, on `ACTIVE`, auto-renew, not a gift and no payment problem. `TERM_DATE_HOLD_IF_LATE`: term date passed and balance 0, so a held one is charged right after the last paid order is created. `TERM_DATE`: term date passed, whatever the balance. `ON_DEPLETION`: balance 0, whatever the date, and a bundle of one can't choose it, because a first release shipped at once would charge a second time within a minute. Both the order and the tick trigger them, and the tick catches a crash in between.

**How a new term is measured (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** from the later of the old term's end and when the charge landed, plus the plan's months. So a held or late renewal buys a whole term, and the next charge can't come sooner than a term after this one. Counting from the old date would make a renewal held for 14 months on a 12-month term due again at once under `TERM_DATE`. A renewal on time drifts by up to an hour a year, from the hourly tick.

**Reach-back, on a bundle:** when a renewal first comes due with a balance of 0, `firstReleaseNumber` moves past every release that has reached its order step. Those are extra and stay out, however long they stay open. A release reached after that point while the payment is failing is a `HELD` Delivery, tied to that release, which ignores `firstReleaseNumber` and ships late once paid. A renewal with a balance left moves nothing, because paid releases are still owed. On recovery the oldest `HELD` Deliveries go as far as the balance covers, and the others are cancelled, since a bundle buys N releases.

**A missed payment, on pay per release (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** one missed payment holds back later releases. When the charge for Issue 5 fails, Issue 5's Delivery is `HELD`. From that first failure, through the retry days and the pause, Issues 6 and 7 get no charge and no Delivery. Recovery is one charge, for Issue 5, which then ships late. Whichever way the problem ends, one batch clears it and moves `firstReleaseNumber` past every release that has reached its order step, so 6 and 7 are skipped for good, even one still open, and the hourly scan can't assign it. That includes a recovery where Issue 5 itself was cancelled, which charges nothing. Issue 8 onward is charged and shipped as normal. A paid balance still ships meanwhile, as it does for anyone paused.

**Failed cards:**

- **Retries:** three tries, on days 0, 3 and 7, then `PAUSED` with `pausedReason` `FAILED_PAYMENT`. `NOT_WORTH_RETRYING` in `app/billing.server.ts` pauses at once for expired or invalid cards, fraud, a bank's challenge and the like. Unknown codes are retried, because gateways often report soft declines generically. A missing card is retried too, since it never reaches a card network. The public "pause or cancel after retries" setting waits for Step 17 (decided by N, 2026-11-04), because it needs a refund decision and a settings screen, so everyone pauses for now.
- **Held:** `Subscription.paymentProblemSince` is set from the first failure, not just the pause. On a bundle, that makes a release reached during retries `HELD`, so it can't be missed by marking it shipped. On pay per release, only the release whose charge failed is `HELD`. `HELD` doesn't count as refused, shows on the releases screen and is in the CSV. Cancelling a release cancels its `HELD` Deliveries, and then nothing is owed for them.
- **Recovery:** `retryCharge`, through the `billing.resume` job. Today the merchant presses Retry charge in Payment problems on the releases screen. **Step 11 calls the same job when a customer updates their card.** A contract with no card also recovers by itself on its next scheduled retry, and since Step 15 on a saved card.

**Chargeback (decided by N, 2026-11-04):** pause and flag, and it holds paid releases too, until the merchant ends it: the customer is disputing what they paid, so shipping more only adds to the loss. This differs from a failed card, which still ships what's paid. `disputes/create` (scope `read_shopify_payments_disputes`, chargebacks only, not inquiries) finds the contract through the order's lines, so it covers the sign-up charge too. `PENDING` Deliveries become `HELD`, new ones are `HELD`, retries stop, and it stays flagged through a cancellation. Only the merchant ends it, with the same button, whichever way the dispute went. On pay per release, ending it lets go what the balance covers, then the oldest `HELD` Delivery left is charged once and the rest are closed, so no second charge follows. Ending it on a cancelled subscription keeps it `CANCELLED` and charges nothing: what's held goes as far as the balance covers, and the rest is closed. A release whose Delivery the cancellation already closed isn't offered again, so that balance ships on the next release, as any cancelled subscriber's does.

**Price changes:** the merchant chooses per change on the plan form, and `updatePlan` records a `PriceChange`. New sign-ups always get the new price, through the selling plan sync. `APPLY_AT_RENEWAL` has an `effectiveAt` 30 days out, for the notice, which is Step 10's and uses `noticeSentAt`. Who it reaches: subscribers on the plan who signed up before `effectiveAt` and whose `lockedPriceCents` differs. Just before a charge's first attempt, once `effectiveAt` has passed, the app updates the contract with a draft (`subscriptionDraftLineUpdate`, per copy, so quantity above 1 stays right) and then `lockedPriceCents`. A retry never does this, so a price change can't apply to a charge already attempted. Only the plan's latest change counts, so a merchant can take one back by saving another with `KEEP_OLD_PRICE`. The 30-day notice is decided by N, 2026-11-04. **Step 10 must send that notice: nothing does yet.**

**Keeping tokens alive:** no separate job. `contracts.reconcile` runs hourly for every shop with a session, and its `unauthenticated.admin` goes through `loadSession`, which refreshes an access token within 5 minutes of expiry. Access tokens last about an hour, so a refresh happens about hourly, and each one starts the 90-day refresh token's clock again (read from D1: `refreshTokenExpires` was about 90 days out). A shop idle for 80 days, or any number of days, is refreshed by the next tick. A dead token (a stopped Worker, say, for over 90 days) logs `session.refresh_rejected` at error level, and every job for the shop fails and goes `DEAD`. Nothing is lost, because all of this is state-driven and job IDs carry the hour: runs, renewals and retries are found again each tick, and the merchant's next page load gets a new token by exchange. The merchant isn't told, though, since the app has no way to reach them until Step 10.

**Events:** `billing.succeeded`, `billing.failed` (`nextRetryAt` null means it paused) and `billing.chargeback`, for Step 10's emails and Flow triggers. Step 10 finds the late-renewal notice's people from `termEndsAt` passed and a balance above 0, so nothing writes an Event for that (see "Emails, Flow and the member tag").

### Emails, Flow and the member tag (2026-11-04)

Technical choices from Step 10 that Steps 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev`, with every email sent to a public test inbox.

**Provider and sender**

- **Cloudflare Email Service** (open beta), behind one function, `sendEmail` in `app/email-send.server.ts`. It needs the Workers Paid plan the app already has: 3,000 emails a month are included and more cost $0.35 per 1,000. Cake Zine's 1,000 subscribers and a few emails per issue fit the 3,000 in any month.
- **One sending address for every shop**, `EMAIL_FROM_ADDRESS` in `wrangler.jsonc` (dev: `no-reply@mail.chill.cool`). The From name is the shop's name and the Reply-To is the shop's contact email, both read from Shopify at send time. Merchants can't send from their own domains, because Email Service needs the domain on Cloudflare DNS. **The production domain is undecided and is N's call before anything is installed on Cake Zine's real store** (Step 16).
- **Limits** (from Cloudflare's docs): up to 50 recipients a message and 25 MiB. A new account starts with a "conservative" daily quota that grows with use, and no figure is published for it or for a per-second rate. So a slice sends 20 emails one after another, and Cloudflare saying `E_RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `E_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` isn't a failure: the slice stops, leaves every row pending with its attempt kept, and the next slice is enqueued 60 seconds later (an hour for the daily limit). Sender or account trouble (`E_SENDER_NOT_VERIFIED`, `E_SENDER_DOMAIN_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `E_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`) waits the same way, because it isn't the customer's fault. Watch `email.waiting` on Cake Zine's first pre-order: if the daily quota is below 1,000, request an increase with Cloudflare's form well before it.
- **No idempotency key**, so the guard against a double send is ours (below).
- **Dev safety:** an optional secret, `EMAIL_RECIPIENT_DOMAINS`, makes the sender mail only addresses at those domains and skip the rest (`recipient_not_allowed`). It is set on the dev Worker to `mailinator.com`, because the dev store's customers have made-up addresses. **Delete it (`wrangler secret delete EMAIL_RECIPIENT_DOMAINS`) when Cake Zine's real store is installed.**

**Exactly once**

- One `EmailSend` row per email per subscription per subject, its ID `kind:subscriptionId:subject` (the subject is a release, an event, a price change, a term date or a Delivery), written before anything is sent. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` makes queueing repeatable. The row holds no names, addresses or text.
- A slice claims a row (`SENDING`, with a lease) just before the provider call, after everything else has been read, and stores the answer after it. What a crash does: **before the claim**, the row is still `PENDING` and goes out later; **between the claim and the stored answer**, the row stays `SENDING`, and once its lease lapses it becomes `UNSURE` and is logged as `email.unsure`. It may or may not have been sent, so it is never sent again by itself. A missing email was judged less bad than a second one. N's decision: the merchant is alerted in the admin and chooses whether to send it again (Step 13). Until then, to send it anyway, set the row back to `PENDING`. **After the answer is stored**, nothing is left to do.
- A hiccup on Cloudflare's side (`E_INTERNAL_SERVER_ERROR`, or an error with no code) puts the row back to `PENDING` for ten minutes later, up to five tries, and then `FAILED`. A refusal (suppressed address, bad content) is `FAILED` at once, with Cloudflare's error code in `reason`. Neither re-sends anything that went.
- **Every email is judged again at send time** (`staleReason`): switched off, subscriber ended or redacted, cancelled with nothing left, release moved on, card fixed, renewed since. It then becomes `SKIPPED` with the reason.
- **Switched-off emails still get a row**, `SKIPPED` (`switched_off`), so turning one back on never sends what was missed. Each email also only goes for something recent (welcome 2 days, pre-order 7, failed payment 3, last release 7, late renewal 7), so a first deploy, an outage or an import doesn't mail old news.
- **State-driven, like a run:** the tick starts an `email.run` job per installed shop every hour (the hour is in its ID); it queues what's due and sends a page of 20, chaining to the next. A merchant's announce and a new sign-up start one at once. A lost message or a crash is found again by the next tick.

**Each email**

- **The price-change notice has no switch**, and is sent even if every email is off. It is what makes N's 30 days' notice real, so letting a merchant turn it off would break the promise. Their lever is the plan form's policy (`KEEP_OLD_PRICE`). `PriceChange.noticeSentAt` is set once all its emails have settled, or at once if nobody needs one. `effectiveAt` is 30 days from saving, so a notice delayed by hours is a little under 30 days. **Charging doesn't check `noticeSentAt`**, because it is set even if rows `FAILED` or went `UNSURE`. Since Step 15, `applyPriceChange` checks each subscriber's own row (see "Hardening").
- **No app shipment email**, and nothing duplicates Shopify's order confirmation on billing orders, as N accepted. The upcoming-charge and failed-payment copy is written knowing the customer also gets Shopify's.
- **Amounts in the upcoming-charge and price-change emails** are what a charge bills: each copy at the line price times the quantity, plus the contract's own `deliveryPrice` (read from Shopify in the slice's one query, together with the customers), and the copy says "plus any tax". A charge's tax wasn't measured, so it isn't stated. A price-change notice gives the old and new totals the same way, so a $15 line with $8 shipping reads $23 to $28. A contract Shopify no longer has gets no email (`no_contract`).
- **Release word and shop name** come from the shop in every email: the words from `Shop.releaseWord*`, the name from Shopify. Copy is in `app/email-templates.ts`, in code. Merchant-editable wording is for Step 17.
- **Personal data:** an address is read from Shopify in the job, in one query per slice, used and dropped; nothing is stored or logged, only IDs. A guest customer (`DISABLED`) still has an address and is reached (checked live). A customer with no address, a deleted one and a `redacted` one get nothing.

**The signed link, and what Step 11 must do with it**

- Buttons go to `/l/<token>` on the app (`app/routes/l.$token.tsx`, public). The token is `base64url(JSON {k, s, r, e}).HMAC`: a kind (`address` or `card`), the subscription ID, the release ID (for an address link) and an expiry 30 days out, signed with a key derived from `SHOPIFY_API_SECRET`. It holds no customer data. **Step 11 replaced the placeholder** (see "Customer portal"): the page records a confirmation only on an explicit press.
- **Step 11 did** replace that page, using `verifyLinkToken` (`app/email-link.server.ts`): for an `address` link, send the customer to the portal's address page and call **`recordAddressConfirmation(rawDb, s, r)`** (`app/emails.server.ts`) when they confirm; for a `card` link, send them to the card page. `recordAddressConfirmation` marks the Delivery for that subscription and release if it exists, and also the pre-order email's `EmailSend` row, because under the default `READY_TO_SHIP` timing the Delivery doesn't exist yet when the email goes out. `createDueDeliveries` copies that mark into the Delivery when it creates it. It keeps the first time.
- **Anyone holding the link can use it until it expires**, such as whoever reads a shared inbox. So it should authorize nothing beyond recording that the address was confirmed, and anything that shows or changes personal data (the address itself, the card) must need the customer to sign in. A card update's `billing.resume` job is as before.
- For gifts (Step 12), the pre-order email should go to the recipient: `EmailSend` goes to the subscription's `customerGid`, so a gift's subscription must carry the recipient there.

Cake Zine is on Basic, so **on a custom install its merchant can't use these triggers at all**. What was checked is that Shopify accepts each trigger's handle and payload through `flowTriggerReceive` and rejects an unknown handle or a missing field.

If Flow can't be reached the claim is given back and the next tick tries again; if the Worker dies between the claim and the answer, that one trigger is lost. **The member discount tag** (`app/member-tag.server.ts`)

- A customer should have `Shop.memberDiscountTag` (default `active-subscriber`) if any of their subscriptions in the shop qualifies: `ACTIVE`, or `CANCELLED` or `PAUSED` with `releasesRemaining` above 0 (paid releases whose orders aren't created). A chargeback, an ended or a redacted subscription never qualifies. It comes off after the last paid release's order is created, which is when its shipping starts. On a plan without auto-renew it stays until the term is up (see "Hardening").
- State-driven: a `member.tag` job per shop per hour compares what each customer should have with `MemberTag`, what the app last put on them, and fixes the difference with `tagsAdd` and `tagsRemove`, which work for guests. A new sign-up gets its own job at once. A renamed tag is moved, and clearing the setting removes the tag. Every step is repeatable. **A tag a merchant removes by hand in Shopify isn't noticed**, because Shopify isn't read, only the app's record.
- **What a merchant must set up in Shopify:** an automatic discount whose customer eligibility is a customer segment such as `customer_tags CONTAINS 'active-subscriber'`. The app only tags. Not tested here.

**GDPR:** `EmailSend` and `MemberTag` are new tables. `customers/redact` deletes the customer's rows from both; `shop/redact` deletes them before the Subscriptions and the Shop; `customers/data_request` lists what was sent (kind, status, time) and never what it said.

### Customer portal (2026-11-04)

Technical choices from Step 11 that Steps 12, 13, 15, 16 and 17 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev`; what couldn't be is listed in the Step 11 report.

**Shape**

- **A customer account UI extension** (`extensions/customer-portal`) shows the portal on the customer's **Profile page** (static target `customer-account.profile.addresses.render-after`, only for customers who have a subscription) and calls the app at `/api/portal` (`app/routes/api.portal.tsx`, logic in `app/portal.server.ts`) with Shopify's session token. Emails and the link page send customers to `https://shopify.com/<shop id>/account/profile` (`portalUrl`). The extension has no environment, so the app's URL is a constant in `src/Portal.jsx` (`API`): change it per deployment.
- **A merchant must place the extension, and that is a step in the admin.** Checked live: before it, a signed-in customer's account showed nothing of ours and loaded a `DeactivatedExtensionsProvider` (a full-page extension's URL, `/account/pages/customer-portal`, answered "There's a problem loading this page" too). The step: Settings, Customer accounts, Customize (the checkout and accounts editor), switch the page to Profile, open Apps, add "Subscription" to Profile, Save. Done on the dev store on 2026-11-04 (a merchant must do it on any store; the Step 17 onboarding should walk them through it). After it, the extension rendered on a real customer's Profile page, after Addresses. A customer with no subscription sees nothing from it. The portal was first built as a full-page extension and moved to a Profile target, which needs no account-menu entry, but the placement step is needed either way. **N's decision (2026-11-04): the portal stays on the Profile page.**
- **The API answers its own preflights.** The extension's `fetch` sends an `Authorization` header, so the browser asks first (OPTIONS). The library's `authenticate` answers a "bot" User-Agent (curl) with a bare 410 before it reaches OPTIONS, and adds no CORS headers to its 401s, so `guarded()` in `api.portal.tsx` answers OPTIONS itself and puts the headers on every answer, errors included. A real request from a bot-looking User-Agent still gets the library's 410, now with the headers.
- **Who is asking** is the token's `sub` (the customer) and `dest` (the shop), and nothing in the request names either. A subscription ID in the body is only ever looked up together with that customer and shop (`ownSubscription`), so another customer's, another shop's, an ended and a redacted subscription all answer 404. A read for a customer with no subscriptions never calls Shopify. **Checked on a real token (the format only):** `dest` is the shop's bare domain (`<shop>.myshopify.com`, no `https://`: parsing it as a URL failed with a 500 until fixed) and `sub` is the customer's GID string (`gid://shopify/Customer/<digits>`), also for a guest (`DISABLED`) customer. The extension's `fetch` from the real Profile page, with its own session token, worked for a read and every action.
- **Nothing personal is stored.** The address and card brand come live from Shopify for the signed-in customer and are not kept or logged.
- **The portal's own copy isn't a per-merchant switch.** AGENTS.md asks for customer-facing copy and emails to be switchable; the emails are. The portal is the place a customer cancels and fixes a card, which a merchant shouldn't be able to switch off (and Shopify's App Store asks for an in-product way to cancel), so it has no switch. Its words come from the shop's release word. **N's decision (2026-11-04): no off switch for the portal.**

**The email link (`/l/<token>`)**

- **The token alone does two things:** shows a page, and on an explicit button press records `addressConfirmedAt`. Opening the page (a mail scanner, a link preview, a prefetch) is a GET and writes nothing; the confirmation is a POST. The page shows the release title and nothing about the customer. Cancelling, changing an address, changing a card and showing the address all need the customer signed in to their account.
- **What a forwarded link can do:** whoever holds it can mark one release's address "confirmed" for 30 days. That flag only tells the merchant who has looked; it changes no address, order or charge, and a later real confirmation or change in the portal overwrites nothing, since the first time is kept. The harm is a merchant believing a subscriber checked when someone else pressed the button. Judged small enough to accept for the convenience of confirming from the email; the portal's own confirm (signed in, with the address shown) is the stronger one.
- **What a subscriber sees as upcoming** is what a run would give them (`upcomingFor`): releases they already have a Delivery on, and releases they are due for by the run's own rule (`DUE_SUBSCRIPTION`), as many as they have paid releases spare. So a cancelled subscriber sees only what they paid for, and a bundle with one release left sees one. A subscriber who signed up during a pre-order has a Delivery on it at once and can confirm. One who is due but has no Delivery and no pre-order email row sees the release but can't confirm, because there is nowhere to keep it.
- **A confirmation counts until the order is created**, because the address is read then: while the subscriber's Delivery is still waiting, or the pre-order email went out and the Delivery doesn't exist yet. A confirmation made before the Delivery exists lives on the email's row and is copied into the Delivery when the run creates it; made after, it goes on the Delivery. Both keep the first time (`recordAddressConfirmation`, `createDueDeliveries`). Changing the address in the portal also confirms what's waiting.
- **Emails offer the portal directly.** The upcoming-charge and price-change emails now have a "Turn off auto-renew or cancel" button straight to the Profile page, not through a signed link: it must keep working after a link would have expired, and it needs the customer's login anyway. "Turn off auto-renew" and "cancel" are the same action, since auto-renew is a plan setting and a subscriber leaves by cancelling.

**Guest customers (state `DISABLED`)**

- A customer who checked out as a guest has a customer record with no account. The contracts and emails belong to that record. **Checked live:** a customer in state `DISABLED` signed in on the dev store with their email and a six-digit code (no password, no account created first), and the account showed that same record's addresses and saved card (a Profile page with the subscription's address and "Test payment gateway •••• 1"). So guests reach the portal by signing in with the email they subscribed with, and the link page says so. Shopify's sign-in code did not reach a Mailinator inbox but did reach a mail.tm one (`fldubai.com`), so use one of those for tests. The real token's `sub` for a guest is a GID like any other's, and the portal worked for one end to end.

**Card**

- **Updating a card is Shopify's email.** `customerPaymentMethodSendUpdateEmail` sends the customer a secure page of Shopify's. The direct `customerPaymentMethodGetUpdateUrl` was checked and only supports Shop Pay, so it can't be the general route. The app never sees a card.
- **Saving a card recovers the payment, with one automatic try (N, 2026-11-04).** The `customer_payment_methods/update` webhook queues `billing.card_updated`, which queues a `billing.resume` job for each of the customer's subscriptions with a payment problem. The job has its own flag, `savedCard`, which is not `byMerchant`: a saved card gets one try that neither the bank-challenge wait nor the hourly limit holds off, while a chargeback hold stays until the merchant ends it (such a subscription is never queued, and `retryCharge` would refuse it anyway). Since Step 15 the job's ID carries a 5-minute window, so two webhooks for one save are one try (see "Decided by N after Step 15"). The portal's "I've updated my card, try again" button queues the same job *without* the flag, so both limits apply to it, and its answer says only what is true whether or not a try follows. **Not seen:** a real `customer_payment_methods/update` webhook. The card email Shopify was asked to send (`customerPaymentMethodSendUpdateEmail`, accepted, no errors) had not reached the test inbox after several minutes, so no card was saved and no real webhook came in on the test gateway; the webhook is proved only by a signed test one (`customer_id` in the payload is an assumption).

**Address**

- The address is written to the contract through a draft (`subscriptionDraftUpdate`), US only (the shop's `allowedCountries`), all fields bounded. It applies to orders created from then on, and the page says so. A pickup contract has no address and shows none.

**Cancel**

- Cancelling cancels the Shopify contract and records it at once (`markCancelled`); the contract webhook repeats it harmlessly. Per "Cancellation" above, **paid releases still ship and nothing is refunded**: checked live, a cancelled contract's paid release still got its order (#1177). What was held back for a failed payment was never paid and is closed, and the page says so. The page states the number still owed in the shop's word before the customer confirms.
- **Refused while a charge is in flight** (a `PENDING` billing attempt), because its result would land after the customer was told they won't be charged. The decisions didn't cover it; this is the conservative choice, and the customer is asked to try again in a few minutes. **N's decision (2026-11-04): keep.**
- **A chargeback hold may be cancelled** by the customer. It stays flagged and held, and both the note before and the message after say the paid releases stay on hold until the shop reviews it.

**Customers can start a charge** (`retryCharge` with `byMerchant` false: a saved card via the webhook, or the portal's "try again")

- **How the Step 9 double charge happens.** A bank's challenge (`ACTION_REQUIRED`) is recorded as a failed attempt, but the challenge can still be completed from Shopify's email, and then that attempt succeeds. A retry in between is a new attempt with its own key, so it charges too. `recordSuccess` credits each success once per attempt, so both credit and the customer pays twice. Before Step 11 only a merchant's press could do this. Now a customer's card update does too, because the card the challenge was for is not the new card.
- **Two rules for the portal's button:** no new attempt while Shopify says a challenge on that charge can still be completed (Step 15 replaced a 3-day guess, see "Double charges" under "Hardening"), and no new attempt once the charge has had 2 attempts in the last hour, however they began, so repeated presses can't hammer a failing card. The merchant's press is held to the first rule and not the second. A customer who is held off recovers when the challenge ends, on the merchant's press, or by saving a new card.
- **A newly saved card skips both rules (N, 2026-11-04).** It retries once, without being the merchant. **A customer who saves a new card and also completes an old bank challenge can be charged twice. N accepted this.** Since Step 15 the second success isn't credited and is flagged for a refund.

### Gifts (2026-11-05)

Technical and product choices from Step 12 that Steps 13, 15, 16 and 17 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` with a real test checkout; what couldn't be is listed in the Step 12 report.

**What a gift is**

- **Only a prepaid bundle can be a gift** (`isGiftPlan`: `giftable` and `PREPAID_BUNDLE`). A per-release plan buys one release and then charges for each later one, which a gift never does, so the plan form refuses "giftable" on one with a message and the picker never offers it. Gift details sent for one anyway were read as the buyer's own purchase until Step 15, and are now flagged like `contract.gift_invalid` (row 15 of the build steps). This is the conservative reading of a giftable per-release plan; the docs didn't settle it, and N accepted it for now and may review it later. (Rows from before this step with `giftable` on a per-release plan stay as they are and are simply never gifts.)
- **The contract is the buyer's, the subscription is the recipient's.** The Shopify contract's customer and card are the buyer's. `Subscription.customerGid` is the recipient's, `giftBuyerGid` is the buyer's and `giftMessage` is the buyer's text. The emails, member tag, Flow triggers, portal and the customer on each $0 release order all follow `customerGid`, so they reach the recipient. The delivery address is the contract's, which is whatever the buyer entered as the shipping address at checkout (the picker tells them to enter the recipient's). The recipient can confirm or change it in the portal.
- **Gift to an existing subscriber** finds their customer by email and gives them a second `Subscription`. Nothing about the customer is changed.
- **A gift never renews and is never charged.** `attemptCharge` refuses `source` `GIFT` before any Shopify call, which is the one choke point for renewals, per-release charges, `retryDue`, `retryCharge` (merchant and customer), `resumeAfterCardUpdate` (also filtered), the lost-result recovery and the price change. The finders and `DUE_SUBSCRIPTION`/`CHARGE_DUE` already left gifts out; each path has its own test. A gift stays `ACTIVE` after its last release (it has nothing to renew into), so the member tag treats a gift with no paid release left as finished.
- **A chargeback on a gift's payment order** still pauses and holds it like any subscription (the buyer disputed what funded it). Unchanged, and the merchant ends it with the same button, which charges nothing.

**How the recipient gets in (storefront to contract)**

- **Line item properties** `Gift recipient email`, `Gift recipient name`, `Gift message`, which Shopify copies onto the contract line as `customAttributes` (checked live: they were on the checkout and the contract). Visible keys on purpose, so the buyer sees what they entered in the cart and checkout. The plan picker shows the fields only for a giftable plan once "This is a gift" is ticked and disables them otherwise, so a recipient typed earlier is never sent with another purchase.
- **Which plans are giftable** reaches the theme through an app-installation metafield, `release_subscriptions.giftable_plans`, written after every plan sync (`publishGiftPlans`) as `,<id>,<id>,` for Liquid to test (checked live: the picker marked only the giftable plan). A failure never blocks the sync.
- **The checkout rule** (`one-plan-per-cart`) also blocks a gift beside any other subscription line (lines of one plan share a contract, so a second recipient would be paid for and lost) and a gift with no valid email, so the buyer is told before paying. Checked live: both answer 422 on `/cart/add.js`. Stores that can't run Functions (Basic, custom app) get only the server-side check below.
- **The server treats it all as untrusted** (`parseGift`): one plain address only (no names, quotes, separators or control characters), a name cleaned and cut to 100 characters, a message cleaned of control characters and cut to 500. A name or message without an email is invalid. The message is plain text everywhere: escaped in the emails and rendered as text by the portal, and the email labels it as written by the sender, not the shop.
- **The recipient's customer** is found by email, else created with the email and name (`customerCreate`). A second run, or two at once, finds the first's customer (Shopify allows an email on one customer; the loser's create is refused and it searches again). The email and name go to Shopify only: the app stores none, logs none, and puts none in a flag.
- **A gift that can't be delivered fails visibly.** If the recipient is missing or invalid, Shopify refuses the address, the gift shares a contract with another line, or its plan isn't giftable, no subscription is created (making the buyer's own would send it to the wrong person), the payment order stays unfulfilled, and an Event `contract.gift_invalid` shows a red banner on the releases screen with the order link and what to do (contact the buyer, then refund the order or cancel the contract in Shopify). "Try again" reads the contract afresh, for when the cause is fixed (see "Hardening"), and "Mark as handled" clears it, shared with the mixed-plans flag. **The buyer is told nothing by the app**, because there is no subscription to send an email for; the checkout rule is what normally stops it.

**Portal and cancel**

- The portal acts on `customerGid`, so only the recipient sees a gift and the buyer sees none of it. A gift shows the recipient its note, the buyer's message (as text), upcoming releases and the address, and **no card, no card actions, no payment problem and no cancel**. The card and "try again" refusals, and the cancel refusal, happen before Shopify is asked anything (`refuseGift`).
- **Cancel on a gift (conservative option, raised for N):** a recipient can't cancel. Cancelling ends the buyer's contract, refunds nothing and still ships every paid release, so it would change nothing for the recipient and touch a contract they don't own. The portal says there is nothing to cancel and to contact the shop if they don't want it. The merchant can still cancel the contract in Shopify.

**Emails and triggers**

| Email | Gift |
| --- | --- |
| Gift received (`giftReceived`, new, own switch, to the recipient, with the message) | Sent after sign-up, with where to sign in to check the address |
| Welcome | Never (`source` is not `NEW`) |
| Pre-order announced | To the recipient, as for any subscriber |
| Last release shipping | To the recipient. For a gift it is also the end-of-gift offer: says it won't renew, that they won't be charged, how to subscribe (the shop's address) and where to sign in. It uses the `lastReleaseShipping` switch, with no switch of its own for the offer |
| Upcoming charge, failed payment, price change | Never, checked at queue time and again at send time |

- The buyer gets nothing from the app. Shopify's own order confirmation on the payment order still goes to them (accepted exception).
- Flow's "Subscription started" and "Last release shipped" go with the recipient's customer ID. The member tag goes to the recipient while paid releases remain.

**GDPR:** `customers/data_request` already matched `customerGid` or `giftBuyerGid`, so a buyer and a recipient each get the gift (with its message) in their export. `customers/redact` for the buyer clears `giftBuyerGid` and the message and leaves the recipient's subscription running; for the recipient it ends the subscription, erases the message, their email rows and their tag. A new `giftReceived` row is an `EmailSend` row like any other.

### Merchant dashboard (2026-11-05)

Technical and product choices from Step 13 that Steps 14, 15, 16 and 17 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev`; what couldn't be is listed in the Step 13 report. Everything here reads records: nothing moves money.

**Revenue per release**

- **Amounts come from Shopify, once, and are kept.** `BillingAttempt` stored no amount and the sign-up payment order wasn't stored at all. An hourly `dashboard.refresh` job per shop (also queued when the Revenue page finds unread payments, at most one per ten minutes) reads each succeeded attempt's order subtotal (`BillingAttempt.amountCents`: a billing order holds only the contract's lines, each at its price times quantity, plus shipping, which the subtotal leaves out) and each subscription's sign-up amount (`Subscription.signupCents`). Both are after discounts and before tax and shipping, as of the read. A checkout order can hold more than the subscription (a one-time product beside the plan, or another plan's contract), so the sign-up amount is never the order's subtotal: it is the discounted total of the origin order's lines whose selling plan is one of this contract's lines' (`signupAmountCents`; the contract line has no link to its order line, and its variant isn't returned, so the selling plan is the match). It is trusted only if the matched lines' quantities add up to the contract lines'; otherwise it stays NULL and shows as unread, never as the subtotal. It only reads what is still NULL, so it is repeatable and a missed hour catches up. **A refund after the read isn't reflected.** Step 14 added `read_all_orders`, so the old limit of 60 days on reading an order is gone. A contract with no checkout order is stored as 0. Migration `0010` clears sign-up amounts stored before this rule, so they are read again. A contract Shopify doesn't return stays NULL and is read again each hour.
- **The rule for assigning a payment to releases.** Every payment buys release slots: a prepaid bundle's sign-up or renewal buys `releasesPerTerm`, a pay-per-release sign-up or charge buys 1. A subscription's shipped releases (Delivery `ORDERED` or `FULFILLED`), lowest release number first, use its slots in the order they were paid for, and each slot is worth its payment divided by the slots that payment bought. So a bundle's payment is recognized as its releases ship, and money for releases not yet shipped is shown as waiting. It doesn't depend on `chargeFor`, so a late, held or skipped release still lines up. A gift's sign-up payment is the buyer's order and counts the same way. Failed and pending attempts buy nothing. What an `IMPORTED` subscription arrived with has no payment in the app's records, so those releases show as unpriced, and a renewal charged here pays for the releases after them (see "Migration import"). One SQL statement with window functions does it (`SLOT_PAYMENTS`).
- **Costs** are per release: product cost per copy, shipping and handling per order, all optional (`Release.productCostCents`, `shippingCostCents`, `handlingCostCents`). Costs count for each shipped order. A release with a cost missing counts it as 0 and the tables say how many orders have no costs, so profit is never silently overstated. Profit is revenue less costs. Shipping is included in the plan price through a free rate (N, 2026-11-03), so the shipping cost is the merchant's. One currency only (the first plan's).

**Who is live**

- A **finished** subscription will never get another release: `ENDED`, `CANCELLED` with none left, or `ACTIVE` at 0 releases remaining that is a gift or on a plan without auto-renew. **Live** is `ACTIVE` and not finished. A pay-per-release plan at 0 isn't finished: it charges on the next release. A cancelled one with paid releases left is "cancelled, still shipping". The subscribers screen's counts, filters and churn all use these (`app/dashboard.server.ts`).
- Checked: the member tag already treats a finished gift as finished; the finders, `DUE_SUBSCRIPTION`, `CHARGE_DUE`, the renewal rules and the upcoming-charge email all leave gifts out. The member tag keeps a non-gift on a plan without auto-renew until its term is up, as N decided (2026-11-05), so someone can be finished here and still hold the tag for the rest of a term they paid for.
- **Churn** is subscribers who cancelled in the last 30 days (`subscription.cancelled` events, which every cancellation path writes through `markCancelled`), gifts left out, over those plus the live ones. Pausing for a failed payment isn't churn. N accepted this definition and the low-stock threshold of 10 (2026-11-05).

**Portal activity** is recorded as `portal.cancelled` and `portal.address_changed` events (no personal data) by the portal's own functions, since nothing durable said how a subscription was cancelled. Cancellations and changes made before this step aren't marked. A confirmation is `Delivery.addressConfirmedAt`, or the pre-order email row's before the Delivery exists, so the confirmations screen lists who was sent the email or holds a Delivery, and who confirmed.

**UNSURE emails** show as a banner on the releases screen with the email, the release and the subscriber, and the warning that sending again may duplicate one that did arrive. "Send again" puts the row back to `PENDING` (only if still `UNSURE`) and queues an email run, which judges it afresh. "Leave it" sets `SKIPPED` with reason `left_unsure`. Neither calls billing.

**Stock** is read hourly by the same job: the lowest `inventoryQuantity` among the plan's product variants that are tracked and don't continue selling out of stock (`Plan.stockLevel`, null when nothing can stop a sign-up). The releases screen warns at 0 or below (sign-ups blocked) and at 10 or below (low). The 10 is a constant, `LOW_STOCK`. A variant that sells past zero is never warned about. The release product isn't checked: release orders ignore stock. It's an hour old at worst.

**Query counts** (`tests/query-count.test.ts`): the releases screen makes 9 D1 statements (shop, releases, run progress, payment problems and their last attempts, flagged sign-ups, UNSURE emails, stock), 7 in parallel after the first two, plus the session read; it was 7 before this step. The subscribers screen's counts and page are one D1 round trip, and Revenue's two report statements are one round trip, with two small reads beside them (4 statements).

### Migration import (2026-11-05)

Technical and product choices from Step 14 that Steps 15, 16 and 17 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` against a made-up order history, because Conjured isn't on any store we can reach; what couldn't be proved is listed under "Saved cards" below and in the Step 14 report. **There is still no sample of Conjured's export**: the columns are inputs on the page, and Step 16 must look at a real one.

**How it runs**

- The page is `/app/import`. The merchant gives the contacts file, the column names, which order lines mean which plan, the releases already shipped, and any gift properties. Nothing is a Cake Zine constant.
- **The file stays in the browser** (`app/import-csv.ts`). The page parses it and sends only each contact's email and whether they cancelled, in batches of 50, to a short request that looks up each email exactly (`customerByIdentifier`) and drops it. Checked live: a malformed address (`foo bar@x`, `a@b`, `x@y@z`, `a@b.co,c@d.co`) comes back `null` with no GraphQL error, beside good ones in the same query, so one bad line is that line's `NO_CUSTOMER`. A customer's email in different case or with spaces around it finds them. Emails reach no table, log or queue message. A report line is known by its customer ID and its line number in the merchant's own file.
- **A dry run** (`import.read`, slices of 20) reads each customer and writes only `ImportRun` and `ImportRow`. **A real run** (`import.create`, slices of 20) marks what the report says can come in, then reads each customer again and acts on that read, so a report is never trusted. A row that has changed is flagged `CHANGED_SINCE_DRY_RUN`, and one that no longer fits is `SKIPPED` with its new category. The tick restarts any run with work left. In either run, a hiccup on one row stops the slice for 60 seconds. Being limited uses no attempts. The same row going wrong 5 times ends the dry run's read of it as `READ_FAILED` and the real run's as `FAILED`, so one bad row can't stop the report or the run from finishing, though it holds up the rows behind it for those 5 minutes.
- **It refuses to start without `read_all_orders`**, because Shopify then silently hides orders over 60 days old and a customer would look as if they never bought.

**Recognizing an imported contract**

- **The marker is on the contract in Shopify:** the contract-level custom attribute `_release_subscriptions_import`, whose value is the row's key (`customer:plan:first order`). `syncContract` checks it before anything else when a contract has no Subscription, and does nothing. So it holds with the webhook first, the D1 row first, and after a crash that left a contract with no row. Shopify copies it onto each billing order's additional details (checked on #1223), as IDs under an underscore key: a merchant can see it there, and themes hide such keys by convention. `contracts.reconcile` still enqueues such a contract, and its job does nothing. Once a Subscription exists, the contract's later webhooks work as for anyone, so a cancellation lands, without the sign-up's email and tag jobs, because 1,000 contracts must not mean 1,000 of each.
- **Checked live: Shopify doesn't dedupe contract creation.** The same input twice made two contracts, and so did `@idempotent` with one key twice (it's accepted and ignored, like `orderCreate`'s). So `importRow` leases the row for 5 minutes, reads the customer's contracts for the marker, creates, then writes the Subscription and the row's result in one batch. **What makes a crash safe is the wait before a retry, and the lease.** A retry comes 60 seconds after an error, or after the 5-minute lease lapses if the Worker died. On the dev store a new contract's marker was back on a read about 5 seconds later, the soonest measured, and Shopify promises nothing about this read, so a second contract would need it to lag past those waits. A customer with more than 50 of this app's contracts is `HISTORY_TOO_LONG`, because a list cut short can't rule out a contract already made.
- **Limits, measured:** 240 contracts on a dev store, 50 to a request, 200 of them in 51 seconds, none refused. So contract creation isn't the bottleneck for 1,000 subscribers. Anything saying slow down is treated as waiting, not failing. Nobody publishes a figure for a paid store.

**Rebuilding the numbers**

- **A purchase** is a line matching the merchant's mapping, on an order that isn't a test, isn't cancelled, is `PAID` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, and still has quantity after refunds.
- **`termEndsAt`** is the last purchase plus the plan's months. **`quantity`** and **`lockedPriceCents`** are the last purchase's: its copies and what they actually paid per copy.
- **`releasesRemaining` carries unused paid releases forward (decided by N, 2026-11-05: "if you were charged for 2 you should get 2").** This replaces last-purchase-only. Within one subscription, `walkBalance` goes through purchases and listed releases in date order, keeping a balance: each purchase adds what it paid for (`releasesPerTerm`, or 1 per release), and each release they received takes 1 off, never going below 0. Two subscriptions on one customer keep a balance each. It's a running balance because a plain "total paid less total received" gets two cases wrong. A **gap**: someone who lapsed and bought again much later didn't get what shipped while their balance was 0, so a release known only by its date isn't theirs and uses up nothing. **Over-shipping**: a free copy seen on an order while the balance was 0 uses up nothing either (`OVER_SHIPPED`). Lines where earlier purchases added to the balance are flagged `CARRIED_OVER`, with the number in the report and CSV, and the report says how many.
- **What counts as received:** the merchant lists each past release: `number | title | date | what its order lines match`. If it has order lines, it counts at the date of a customer's order holding one, and with none it doesn't count, and `ISSUE_NOT_FOUND` says so if they had a balance when it shipped. Without order lines it goes by date: shipped during their term while they had a balance means it was theirs (`DATES_ONLY`). **Counting by date stops at the end of the latest purchase's term (decided by N, 2026-11-05: "stop counting issues after term end", because an extra copy beats shorting someone).** A release known only by its date that shipped at or after that moment takes nothing off and isn't received, and if a balance was left, the line is flagged `SHIPPED_AFTER_TERM`. A later purchase starts a new term. A plan with no term isn't affected, and a release seen on an order counts whenever it came, because an order is proof. **The cost:** on a date-only list, someone Conjured did keep shipping to after their term comes in owed an issue, and on hold. With order lines listed this doesn't arise, so a real export should list them where it can. The date rule can't see a subscriber who was sent the latest issue on signing up, so it leaves them owed one more, which ships an extra copy rather than short-changing anyone.
- **A list that doesn't reach back (`HISTORY_BEFORE_LIST`):** a purchase from before the earliest listed release can't be counted, because what it was used for can't be seen. So the walk starts at the first purchase inside the list, or at the last purchase if none is, and flags the line, with how many weren't counted. The page says the list should reach as far back as purchases do, and shows how many lines are affected. Starting at the last purchase before the list instead would credit it in full, when its term probably used it on releases nobody listed: for a long-standing subscriber that's up to a whole term's worth free, across everyone, and each one's renewal then waits for it. This way, what a short list costs is carried releases that adding lines and running again gets back.
- **No `Release` rows for those issues:** the real run writes them, as `SHIPPED`, with the merchant's numbers. Without them the app's next release would be number 1, and every `firstReleaseNumber` would keep imported subscribers out of it forever. A shipped release is closed, so nothing is ever ordered for them. A number held by a release that hasn't shipped refuses the run. New sign-ups under `PREORDER_SWITCHOVER` now get the latest of these as their first release, which is Cake Zine's rule, so **Step 16 must choose a product on it** or their order waits, visibly, with no product.
- **`firstReleaseNumber`** is past the last listed release and past anything at its order step, so the hour after an import makes no order. A paid subscriber kept out of a release already shipping keeps their balance for the next (`OPEN_RELEASE_SKIPPED`).
- **Two subscriptions:** per plan, an order continues the subscription whose term ends within 60 days after it, or has ended. Any other order is a second subscription, with its own contract. A plan with no term can't be told apart, so it's one. They're flagged for a human.

**Nobody is charged because of the import**

Who'd be charged too soon or with no notice comes in `PAUSED` with `pausedReason` `IMPORT_HOLD`. It's the existing no-charge state: `renewalDue`, `findRenewals`, `CHARGE_DUE`, `chargeRelease` and `attemptCharge` all need `ACTIVE` (or a `FAILED_PAYMENT` pause), and paid releases still ship, as for anyone paused. It isn't a card problem, so the pre-order email keeps its address button and it stays out of Payment problems.

| Case | What the importer does | Category |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Term over, nothing left | No contract: nothing to ship, and a renewal would charge within the hour | `LAPSED` |
| Term over, paid releases left, carried ones included | Held. Under `TERM_DATE` it would charge within the hour; under the hold rule, at the next order with no notice, because the late notice only goes within 7 days of the date | `HOLD_TERM_PAST` |
| Renewal under 21 days away | Held, because it would miss the notice. It also gives the merchant at least 21 days after the import to switch Conjured off for everyone not held | `HOLD_RENEWAL_SOON` |
| `ON_DEPLETION` at 0, or at 1 with a pre-order already open | Held: `renewalDue` ignores the date | `HOLD_DEPLETED` |
| Last purchase cost nothing | Held: it would renew for nothing. Releasing it doesn't fix that, and the banner says so: the contract's price stays $0 | `HOLD_ZERO_PRICE` |
| Pay per release at 0 | Comes in live, flagged. Charged when the next release ships, as any such subscriber is, with its notice at the pre-order. Kept out of a pre-order already open, whose notice has gone (`PREORDER_SKIPPED`) | `READY` |

**Releasing a hold** is a button on the subscriber's page, and needs the merchant to have checked Conjured has stopped. A term date under 22 days away moves out to 22 days from now, so the dated notice goes first and nothing can charge without it. This is the one place `termEndsAt` isn't the real date, and the subscriber gets a slightly longer term for free. `ON_DEPLETION` has no date to move and charges within the hour with no notice, and the button says so. **Holds aren't lifted one at a time at scale**: if Step 16 finds more than a few, it needs a bulk button.

**Double billing with Conjured**

The importer reads Shopify's orders and never reads or touches Conjured's contracts (it can't: `read_own_subscription_contracts`). Term dates are as of the read, and the report's banner says so with the time. A subscriber who renews there before the real run gets the new date. **One who renews there after the real run isn't noticed by anything but another dry run**, which flags `RENEWED_SINCE_IMPORT` and changes nothing. So Step 16 must switch Conjured off before the first app charge, run a dry run just after it, and read that flag.

**Contract details**

- The line's selling plan is the plan's, on the first variant of the plan's product, which never ships. Its price is what they paid. `deliveryPrice` is always 0: it sticks and bills every renewal, and shipping is included through a free rate. Someone who paid shipping before is flagged `PAID_SHIPPING`. Billing and delivery policies come from `cycleOf`, as a checkout's do. `nextBillingDate` is the term date, or a year out if that's past or there isn't one, and it's only a label.
- **Address:** the customer's default, else their last purchase's. Checked against `allowedCountries`. A missing one is `NO_ADDRESS` and one outside is `ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED`, and neither gets a contract. Only the country is kept.
- **Cancelled with releases owed** gets a contract created as `CANCELLED`, with no card, and a `CANCELLED` Subscription, so paid releases ship and nothing can charge. Checked live: Shopify accepted it, and the run made its order from the cancelled contract's address (#1241).
- **Price changes:** an imported subscriber signed up after a rise was saved never got its notice, so `applyPriceChange` leaves them on what they pay (`PRICE_CHANGE_PENDING` in the report). A rise saved after the import reaches them with its notice like anyone's.
- **No card:** the contract is created with none, the row and the subscriber's page say so, the subscribers screen has a red banner and a filter (`noCardSince`), and the charge goes out and fails as `PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SPECIFIED`, with the retries and pause it has today. Nothing would otherwise ever put a card on such a contract, so `attemptCharge` attaches the customer's saved card just before a charge if they now have one. The portal's "update card" can't help someone with no card, and `customer_payment_methods/create` wasn't subscribed to, so a card saved after the last retry waited for the merchant's Retry charge. Since Step 15 that webhook is subscribed to, and the saved card recovers it (see "Decided by N after Step 15").

**A carried balance and renewals**

Under `TERM_DATE_HOLD_IF_LATE`, a balance above 1 means the renewal keeps waiting until the last paid release's order, as `renewalDue` has it. Under `TERM_DATE` it charges at the term date whatever the balance, and the charge adds to the balance, so nothing carried is lost. Two notices were wrong for this, and were changed for every subscriber, not only imported ones. The dated notice ("On this date we'll charge") no longer goes under the hold rule with 2 or more left, because it wouldn't be true. And a renewal already on hold now gets the per-release notice with its last paid release's pre-order, because the charge can come long after the only notice that went at the term date. Under `PREORDER_OPENS` Shopify's receipt is still the notice, as for any per-release charge.

**Also decided by N, 2026-11-05:** a no-card contract takes the customer's saved card just before a charge. Lapsed subscribers get no contract, renewals under 21 days away are held, cancelled subscribers still owed issues get them and are never charged or refunded, and the date rule can ship an extra copy.

**Who stays out**

**Already imported under a key that has changed:** the marker is `customer:plan:first order`, so correcting a plan mapping, or a first order later being refunded or cancelled, changes the key, and an exact match alone would make a second contract and charge them twice. So if a customer has a contract with a marker that none of this read's keys explains, any of their chains with no exact match is `IMPORTED_UNDER_ANOTHER_KEY`: no contract, in the report, in the dry run and the real run's fresh read alike. An early renewal that starts a new chain keeps its old key among those found, so it's still a second subscription, and a second plan added to the mapping still comes in. **To fix a wrong mapping after an import, the merchant has to deal with the existing contract and Subscription by hand: nothing moves them to the right plan.**

Each of these has a category and no contract: `IMPORTED_UNDER_ANOTHER_KEY`, `READ_FAILED`, `NO_CUSTOMER`, `DUPLICATE_CONTACT` (two lines for one customer; if they disagree, cancelled wins, so neither charges), `NO_ORDERS`, `UNRECOGNIZED_PLAN`, `HISTORY_TOO_LONG`, `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED_NOTHING_OWED`, `GIFT`, `ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED`, `NO_ADDRESS`, `REDACTED`, `ALREADY_SUBSCRIBED` (a live subscription on that plan from this app's checkout), `CURRENCY_DIFFERS`, `PLAN_NOT_READY`. **Gifts bought in the old app stay out and are handled by hand for now (decided by N, 2026-11-05).** `source` can't be both, and importing one would need recipient details the order history doesn't reliably give. New gifts are unaffected. **An export with no status column** flags everyone `STATUS_UNKNOWN`, puts a red banner on the report and needs a tick before the real run, because someone who cancelled with a term running then looks active. Their protection is the upcoming-charge notice.

### Hardening (2026-11-05)

Technical choices from Step 15 that Steps 16 and 17 live with. Checked on `newstand-dev` on the test gateway, and against made-up shops in the dev D1 that no hourly finder can see. What couldn't be checked is in the Step 15 report.

**Double charges**

- **What Shopify lets us ask (read from its schema, API 2026-10):** `subscriptionBillingAttempt(id)` has a `state` that is one of `Pending`, `ActionRequired`, `Failed` or `Success`. `ActionRequired` has an `action`, whose `status` is `OFF_SESSION_REJECTED` (the customer hasn't tried yet) or `ON_SESSION_CHALLENGED` (they're on it). A failed one can carry `EXPIRED_BUYER_ACTION`, so challenges do seem to expire, though nothing says when and it hasn't been seen. **There is no mutation to call a challenge off:** `subscriptionBillingAttemptCreate` is the only billing attempt mutation. So all we can do is choose whether to try again.
- **Before any new try, Shopify is asked about the charge's challenged attempts** (`askAboutChallenges`, from `retryCharge` and `retryDue`). If one has paid, it is credited and no new try is made, which also recovers a lost webhook. If one is still open, nothing is tried, with one exception: a newly saved card goes ahead (N, 2026-11-04). This replaces the 3-day guess, so a customer's button now waits as long as Shopify says, and works straight away once a challenge has failed or expired.
- **A merchant's press now waits for an open challenge too.** This departs from "a merchant's press ignores both rules" in Step 11, on purpose: that was the known hole, and the merchant can't see a challenge. The Payment problems row says why it's waiting. They can have the customer save a new card, or wait for the challenge to end.
- **A second success on one charge isn't credited.** A charge is `subscriptionId` and `chargeFor`, so a release or a term. The batch that credits checks no attempt on that charge has `SUCCEEDED`, in each statement, so two arriving together can't both credit. The second becomes `BillingAttempt.result` `DUPLICATE`, keeps its order ID and writes a `billing.double_charge` Event, which shows a red banner on the releases screen with the order to refund. Its payment order is still marked fulfilled, because it never ships. It buys no slots in the revenue report, and "Charged twice" shows on the subscriber's page. The app refunds nothing.
- **A failure landing after another try paid changes nothing:** no problem, no retry, no failure email, no pause. Before this, it started the retries on a paid charge.
- **Still possible, and flagged rather than prevented:** a customer completing a challenge in the moment between our read and the new try, or at any time after a saved card's try.
- **Not a double charge:** a challenge completed on a release that has since been cancelled, when a later release was charged. They paid for one release each time, so it credits a release for the next run.
- `retryCharge` now needs `paymentProblemSince`, so a crafted portal request can't start a charge from an old failed attempt on someone with nothing wrong.

**Price rises**

`applyPriceChange` refuses a rise unless this subscriber's own `priceChange` email row is `SENT`, at least 29 days ago (the rise is saved 30 days ahead and the notice goes on the next hourly run). That covers `FAILED`, `UNSURE`, skipped and pending rows, a notice sent late, a sign-up in the plan sync's lag at the old price, and an import after the rise, which had a rule of its own until now. They keep what they pay until a later change reaches them. A price coming down needs no notice. The upcoming-charge email quotes what the charge will really take by the same rule, and now also respects `KEEP_OLD_PRICE`, which it used to ignore. `noticeSentAt` is unchanged and nothing that charges reads it. A later slice of an email chain no longer marks it, because that could mark a rise saved after the chain's rows were queued, so that its notices were never queued at all.

**The member tag**

On a non-gift plan without auto-renew it stays until `termEndsAt`, then the hourly job takes it off (N, 2026-11-05). Past the term it stays while a paid release is still owed, as for a cancelled subscriber, because `docs/PLAN.md` says the tag goes after the last paid release ships. N confirmed this reading (see "Decided by N after Step 15"). With no term, as on a per-release plan, it goes with the last paid release.

**Other charging rules**

- **No address, no charge:** a first try at a release's charge with no address on the contract charges nothing and sets `Delivery.orderError`, so it shows as a refused order, and choosing the product again retries it. A retry isn't checked, and a bundle's renewal isn't, because it buys no one order, so those still end in a visibly refused order.
- **Cancelling a release** whose charge had paused a pay-per-release subscriber ends the problem at once, with what Retry would have done: `ACTIVE`, flag cleared, moved past reached releases. Another `HELD` release, a chargeback or a bundle's unpaid renewal keeps it as it is.
- **A contract reactivated in Shopify** at a balance of 0 is moved past every release at its order step, so a release open right now can't charge them with no notice. With a balance it stays where it is, and a chargeback goes back to `PAUSED`.
- **A contract paused in Shopify when a retry comes** keeps its payment problem, so the merchant still sees the unpaid renewal. Before this it was cleared, and the bundle then did nothing, unflagged.
- **The per-release notice has no 7-day limit any more:** someone paused when the pre-order opened, who recovers weeks later, is still charged when it ships, and is told first.

**Sign-ups**

- A `contract.sync` job reaching the dead-letter queue with no Subscription writes `contract.sync_dead`, a red banner with Try again. Try again is the same job under a new ID, for this and for `contract.gift_invalid`. The flag clears when a Subscription is created, whichever way that happens.
- What a merchant can correct for a gift is limited, because the admin can't edit a contract line's properties: create the customer, or make the plan giftable. So Try again mostly helps a refused address.
- **Rate limiting gifts per buyer was considered and not done:** each gift is a paid charge, so using the shop to email strangers costs the sender the plan's price each time, and a limit would catch a real buyer's Christmas list. N accepted this (see "Decided by N after Step 15").

**Plan sync**

When a plan comes off a product, `requiresSellingPlan` is reset first and the plan removed after, because a retry only sees a product while the plan is still on it. A sync dying in between leaves a product that can be bought one time for a moment, rather than one that can't be bought at all. `sellingPlanGroupsCount` therefore counts this plan's group at that point.

**Handing over a data request**

The releases screen shows a banner per `gdpr.data_request`, with Download (`/app/data-request/<id>`, a JSON file) and Handed over, which deletes the copy here, because it can hold a gift message and nothing else needs it. The file now also lists the member tag, the subscription's events and what each payment brought in. `customers/redact` also removes the customer's `billing.card_updated` job rows, whose keys hold their ID.

**Rate limits**

`RateLimit` in D1 (`app/rate-limit.server.ts`), because Workers share no memory between isolates. A row is a hash of the limiter and who, a count and when the window ends, so no customer ID or IP is kept, and rows go a day after. Per customer per hour: read 120, confirm 30, address 30, retry 10, card email 5. **Cancel has no limit**, because a customer is owed it. The link page's confirm is 20 per hour per IP per subscription, so whoever holds a forwarded link uses up only their own presses and can't lock the customer out, and the signed-in portal is a second way to confirm. A press that is limited changes nothing, and confirming was idempotent anyway.

**Pruning**

A daily `maintenance.prune` job, at 03:00 UTC, removes what can no longer matter, with the job row's key the date so a repeat does nothing.

| Table | Removed | Kept, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `JobRun` | `DONE` and lapsed `CLAIMED` over 30 days, `DEAD` over 90 | **All of `contract.sync`.** The hourly check enqueues every contract with no Subscription under its revision, and this row is what stops one the merchant has dealt with being read again, and perhaps becoming a subscription after a refund |
| `ImportRow` | Not `DONE`, over 30 days old, in a finished run with a newer run after it | `DONE` rows, which every later dry run compares against for `RENEWED_SINCE_IMPORT`, and the latest and any running report |
| `RateLimit` | Windows that have ended | |

A queue keeps a message at most 14 days and Shopify retries a webhook for hours, so nothing older than 30 days can still arrive as a repeat. Every other job ID either holds the hour or a random key, or its job reads state afresh and changes nothing if it repeats (checked job by job, `plan.sync`, `payment_order.fulfill`, `delivery.order` and `billing.settle` being the ones with stable IDs).

**Also fixed**

- The Flow slice no longer chains straight back to the same events when Shopify is down. It stops at the first failure and waits for the tick.
- The dashboard's hourly read pages by ID, so contracts and orders Shopify doesn't return no longer hold up the rest.

**Timing (seeded, then deleted):** every page at 1,000 subscribers, 4,000 Deliveries, 2,000 attempts, 3,100 Events and 4,000 email rows: subscribers 5 ms in D1, Revenue 37 ms (the slowest, 74,000 rows read), confirmations 11 ms, subscriber detail 2 ms, releases 5 ms. Nothing needed changing. The releases screen is still 9 statements, because every new flag comes from the query it already had.

**`deepmerge-ts`** comes only through `prisma` to `@prisma/config`, the CLI's config loader. It isn't in the Worker: a search of the built `build/server` finds neither it nor `@graphql-tools` or `lodash`, the other audit findings. Prisma stays on 6.

The alternative, the app publishing its plans' product handle, needs a second metafield for a shop that has one such product. If the setting is empty the block renders nothing, and says why in the theme editor.
- **Same plans, same gifts:** the plan list and the gift fields come from one Liquid snippet, `plan-options`, used by the plan picker too, and `plan-picker.js` handles the gift fields in both: giftable plans only, recipient email required while ticked (even on a `novalidate` theme, because the module's form has no `novalidate`), fields disabled unless ticked. Gift properties are sent in the add request.
- **Wording:** the block's own text (heading, button, terms) is merchant-editable and says neither "issue" nor any release word, so there is none to hard-code. The success and error lines are in the extension's locale file. **The off switch is the theme editor's:** remove or disable the block, which needs no app setting.
- **The theme's cart:** if the page has a `cart-drawer` or `cart-notification` element with `getSectionsToRender` and `renderContents` (Dawn's), the module asks for those sections in the add request and renders them, so the drawer opens. Any other theme gets "Added to your cart. View cart". Only Dawn's was tried.

**A tier is a plan with a level, not a separate thing.** Billing, issues per term, the term, renewal and gifts all belong to the plan already; a tier only adds what it includes and its own tag. Issue-count plans and tiers sit side by side: an issue-count plan is a plan with no tag or perk text. A rank (for the picker's order and which way a change goes) waits for 16d.

**What was built**

- **Perk text: `Plan.description`.** Plain text up to 500 characters, keeping line breaks, with no `<`, `>` or control characters. It is sent as the selling plan's `description` and read back by the sync's compare ("Selling plan description"). Shopify reads an empty one back as null, and both mean none. The picker shows it under the plan's name (`plan-options.liquid`, escaped, `newline_to_br`). It is editable on the plan form.
- **Tags per plan: `Plan.memberTag`.** A customer's wanted tags are the shop's member tag if any of their subscriptions qualifies, plus each qualifying subscription's plan tag, by the same `QUALIFIES` rules as before. So a tier's tag comes off when that tier stops qualifying, and the shop's stays for another subscription. `MemberTag.tag` now holds that set, sorted and comma-separated (Shopify tags hold no commas), so a row written before this, holding one tag, is already in that form. The SQL builds it with `group_concat(tag, ',' ORDER BY tag)`, which D1 and the test runtime both support (checked). Only the difference is sent: one `tagsRemove` and one `tagsAdd` with lists. A plan tag that's also the shop's tag is given once. **Use tags only this app adds:** a tag another app or Flow also adds (such as the old Flow's `subscriber`) would be removed by this app when it stops qualifying. The plan form says so.
- **Vocabulary: `Shop.vocabulary`, `SUBSCRIPTIONS` (default) or `MEMBERSHIPS`,** in Settings next to the release word. A preset rather than free words, so the copy stays grammatical. Each one fixes the noun (subscription or membership), the person (subscriber or member) and the verb (subscribe or join): `termsOf` in `app/vocabulary.ts`. Existing shops read `SUBSCRIPTIONS`, so nothing changed for them, and anything unknown reads the same.
  - **It covers:** the portal (its heading, banners, cancel buttons and error messages, now sent from the server's `copy`), the email link page, every email (including the footer, "you are a member of"), the picker's fallback legend, and the selling plan group's option name ("Subscription" or "Membership").
  - **How the picker knows:** the app-installation metafield `release_subscriptions.vocabulary`, written after each plan sync (`publishVocabulary`).
  - **Changing it:** saving a different preset re-syncs every plan of the shop, which moves the groups' option name and the metafield. The group update now always sends `options`.
  - **Not covered yet:** admin labels.
- **Import: an order tag (`orderTag`).** When set, a shipped release on an order counts as received only if the order carries that tag, case-insensitive, so a copy a member bought in the shop isn't read as one of their paid issues. Empty keeps today's behaviour, and runs saved before it read as empty. Cake Zine: `subscriber`, which the old app's Flow put on members' sign-up orders and the bulk issue orders put on theirs. The import now reads each order's `tags`.
- **Import: the date the list covers from (`listFrom`), for N's answer 4.** Before this, a purchase dated before the first listed release was outside the list. For a member with only that purchase the walk already started at it, so their balance was right (a March 2026 sign-up: +2, Steak Zine −1, Volume 8 −1). But every such line was flagged `HISTORY_BEFORE_LIST`, about 140 of Cake Zine's 290, and an earlier purchase in the same chain was dropped. With `listFrom` set to the day memberships began, purchases from then are inside the list: counted in full and not flagged. It asserts that nothing shipped to subscribers between that day and the first line, so it is the merchant's call, and empty keeps today's behaviour. Cake Zine: 2026-02-01.
- **A first issue on the sign-up order still counts.** The old Flow added the latest issue to each new member's sign-up order, and the walk puts a purchase before a release at the same moment (tested).
